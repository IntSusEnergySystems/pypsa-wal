## 3. The TIMES-priced CO₂ disposal route (decided 2026-10-04)

### 3.1 Why

On 1 Oct `scen_noccsccgt` barely differed from the central (R3): the central already built no
CCGT CC.
* The cause is the endogenous disposal price: 347 €/t at BEWAL in 2050, after lever A made
  CO2StoP storage scarce.
* At that price capture on gas power does not pay (2050 price swap: 24 % capex recovery at
  1 Oct CO₂ prices, 211 % at 13 Sep's 78 €/t).

TIMES-WAL never sees that price. It buys disposal as a per-tonne service at 64–84 €/t, with a
volume cap, and keeps its 1.02 GW CCGT CCS retrofit (1.86 Mt/a from 2040, §7.1).

Sylvain's design:
* **central**: Wallonia may use TIMES's route at TIMES's price;
* **noccs**: no power-plant capture **and** endogenous disposal only.

### 3.2 What TIMES-WAL does (read from the 2 Oct central `.vd`)

| leg | TIMES process | how TIMES carries it | per tonne, €2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walloon onshore collection + backbone to the regional border | `STORAGEMININD` / `STORAGEMINELC` | investment + FOM + VOM: (Cost_Inv + Cost_Fom + Cost_Act) / VAR_Act = 11.76 TIMES-€/t for every vintage | **13.8** |
| downstream: Antwerp liquefaction terminal, shipping, offshore storage (ship-or-pay) | `CO2STG01` | VOM, Cost_Act / VAR_Act = 59.6 / 49.4 / 46.0 / 42.6 TIMES-€/t | **70 / 58 / 54 / 50** (2035 / 40 / 45 / 50) |
| **full chain** | | | **83.8 / 71.8 / 67.8 / 63.8** |
| volume cap | `CO2STG01` bound | binds at 6 000 kt (2040) and 7 000 kt (2045); 6 983 kt in 2050 under 8 000 | 4 / 6 / 7 / 8 Mt/a |

* The deflator, 0.8516 TIMES-€ per €2025, is the one that turns the convergence CSV's
  70 / 58 / 50 €2025 into the `.vd`'s 59.6 / 49.4 / 42.6. Annick's "~12 €/t" for the onshore leg
  (18 Sep e-mail) is the TIMES-currency figure.
* Before 2035 TIMES has neither a tariff nor any activity (no `CO2STG01` row in 2030).
* **TIMES's own shadow price** at `CO2STOCK` (EQ_CombalM): 58 / **109** / **149** / 43
  TIMES-€/t in 2035 / 40 / 45 / 50. In 2040 and 2045 the cap binds, and a scarcity rent of
  ~60–100 €/t sits on top of the tariff.

### 3.3 Is it compatible with PyPSA? Yes, as a node-level service, with two guards

* **No double counting of transport.** The tariff covers transport to the store. So the route is
  a sink at BEWAL, the existing `BEWAL co2 sequestered` Link + Store, and uses no PyPSA CO₂
  pipeline. PyPSA charges no intra-node collection anywhere, so the onshore leg is added on top,
  as TIMES does.
* **Myopic capital amnesia.** A capital cost on the store vintage would be paid only in its
  build year. The price is therefore a **marginal cost per tonne on the sequestration Link**,
  paid every year at that horizon's tariff, which is what a service is. The Store's capital cost
  is set to 0.
* **Only Walloon CO₂.** The route is cheaper than the endogenous price in 2050. Without a guard,
  Flemish and foreign CO₂ would be piped into BEWAL to use it; the toy LP of
  `test_co2_disposal_service.py` shows the leak.
  * New solve-time row `co2_disposal_own_capture_BEWAL`: annual flow into the route ≤ CO₂ that
    BEWAL's own links put on `BEWAL co2 stored`, i.e. capture ports with positive efficiency.
  * Local use (FT, methanolisation) may still draw on imported CO₂.
  * Transit through Wallonia is untouched.
  * The dual is stored as a GlobalConstraint.
* **It is an export route, not Walloon geology.** Physically it stands for a subscription to a
  shared chain such as Antwerp → Northern Lights (or Aramis). The documented Belgian zero of
  `co2 storage` (no CO2StoP site) stays; the route is written over it after the potentials loop.
  Northern Lights is in Norway, outside the six modelled countries, so the 8 Mt do not compete
  with the DE/NL/GB CO2StoP ceilings (188.2 Mt/a system-wide since lever A). Strictly, Aramis is
  Dutch and would.
* **The endogenous route stays open.** Above the cap, Walloon CO₂ can still leave by pipeline at
  the endogenous price, as on 1 Oct. Reporting: the tonnes appear as `BEWAL co2 sequestered`,
  which must be read as "exported through the TIMES route", not stored in Wallonia.

### 4.1 BEWAL 2040 cap: why 15 %, and what it mimics

* TIMES-WAL reaches 6 826 kt in 2040 (2 Oct central), −85 % against its 45.6 Mt reference. It
  gets there under **both** a CO₂ constraint and an exogenous carbon price (ETS1/ETS2
  trajectories). Annick's 18 Sep tests showed that the price trajectory, more than the bound,
  drives TIMES's abatement.
* PyPSA has no exogenous Walloon carbon price. Its Walloon carbon price is the dual of the
  constraint plus the six-country cap. With the shared 25 % fraction, 2040 was loose: 11.4 Mt,
  dual −7 €/t, gas power 1.9× TIMES's.
* **We mimic TIMES's price-driven 2040 abatement with the constraint alone**, set to TIMES's own
  tonnage: 6 826 / 45 600 = 15.0 %.
* It applies to every scenario of the batch, whatever its own TIMES 2040 (NoCCSCCGT 7.29 Mt,
  RetardNucl. 6.77, réaliste 7.20). It is a cap, not a pin.
* Other regions and the system `co2_budget` keep 25 %. 2030 (45 %) and 2050 (5 %) are unchanged.
* On the 5-year grid BEWAL 2045 would interpolate the shared series (15 %), and 2035 stays at
  35 %: a 20-point step from 2035 to 2040 for BEWAL. Revisit before any 5-year run.

**Feasibility of the 6.84 Mt cap (approximate check, 4 Oct).** The national cap is hard: no
vent, no load shedding, no DAC, no Walloon geology, and the electricity import cap binds. The
check takes the solved 1 Oct 2040 central (11.40 Mt, binding) and asks whether each scenario
has enough abatement levers to remove the extra 4.56 Mt. Lever sizes are read from the network
(`tmp/feas2040/levers.py`, `dispatch.py`).

| lever (BEWAL 2040) | size | allowed in |
|---|---:|---|
| fixed by TIMES demands: land-transport oil 1.75, HVC 1.05, coal 0.40, process residual 0.36, agriculture/shipping 0.20 | 3.8 Mt **not abatable** | — |
| unabated CCGT 29.97 TWh gas → `CCGT CC` (residual 0.01 t/MWh, unlimited capacity, route or pipelines for the CO₂) | ≈ 5.6 Mt | all but the two noccs |
| `gas for industry` 7.53 TWh → `gas for industry CC` | ≈ 1.42 Mt | all |
| Walloon biogas 4.0 TWh (cap) → gas grid, −0.198 t/MWh; now cheap (§4.3) | ≈ 0.79 Mt | all |
| gas CHP → CHP CC | ≈ 0.45 Mt | all but the two noccs |
| more PV: CCL slack 13.17 − 9.53 = 3.6 GW new build (ground potential 12.6 GW free, CF 0.111) replacing CCGT at 0.338 t/MWh_e | ≈ 1.1–1.2 Mt | all |
| `H2 turbine` / `H2 Fuel Cell` on piped hydrogen: unlimited capacity, ~3.7 GW of existing H2 pipelines into BEWAL, outside the electricity import cap | backstop for all of the CCGT's 5.9 Mt | all |

* **CC allowed** (central, retardnucleaire, biomethane, réaliste): ≈ 9.5 Mt available
  without hydrogen against 4.56 needed. **Feasible with a >2× margin.**
  * retardnucleaire's nuclear delay acts only from 2045.
  * réaliste enters 2040 with less PV and more gas because its 2030 is uncapped. It has the
    same levers and a larger PV build allowance.
* **`scen_noccsccgt` and `scen_noccsccgt_route`:** industry CC + biogas + PV slack ≈ 3.4 Mt.
  The remaining ≈ 1.2 Mt must come from hydrogen turbines. That replaces ≈ 3.4 TWh_e of CCGT
  output with ≈ 7 TWh/a of H₂, a fifth of the existing pipeline capacity, or partly from
  heat-pin buy-outs. **Feasible, but expensive.**

### Findings

* **F1. The route works as designed and reshapes 2050, not 2040.** Closed in 2025–2030 (`closed this horizon`), 6 Mt available in 2040 at 71.8 €/t **but unused** (0 Mt in every scenario, consistent with §3.5's expectation that the route is *dearer* than the endogenous price in 2040; the 2040 endogenous price itself was not extracted), full 8 Mt in 2050 at 63.8 €/t in the five scenarios that have it. The prices explain both: the endogenous `BEWAL co2 stored` price is **−47.6 €/t in 2040** (below the 71.8 €/t tariff, so the route stays empty) and **−332 €/t in 2050** (far above 63.8, so the 8 Mt is filled and is worth about 268 €/t × 8 Mt ≈ 2.1 bn€ of rent, the +2.1 bn€ of the disposal-price effect of F8). The endogenous export from Wallonia disappears in 2050 (`BEWAL co2 sequestered` = 8 Mt, no pipeline export), exactly as the 1 Oct design intended. **Reporting caution:** `BEWAL co2 sequestered` is an *export subscription*, not Walloon geology.
* **F2. The own-capture row has a non-zero dual in `scen_noccsccgt_route` 2050 (−131 €/t); it is 0 in every other scenario.** The row `co2_disposal_own_capture_BEWAL` is therefore binding there: with power-plant capture banned, the LP would pay up to 131 €/t to feed the route with CO₂ captured outside Wallonia, and the guard prevents it. This is the case the guard was written for. **Checked on the network:** in the twin the own-capture total (capture ports with positive efficiency on `BEWAL co2 stored`) is **exactly 8.0 Mt**, equal to the route flow and to the 8 Mt volume cap, plus 0.39 Mt of local use (FT). The two limits therefore **tie** and the −131 €/t is a degenerate split of one binding pair: do not quote it as the value of Flemish CO₂. In the other scenarios own capture is above the flow (central 8.36 Mt, biomethane 8.64, réaliste 8.92, retardnucleaire 11.27) and the row is slack; noccsccgt has no route (own capture 7.37 Mt).
* **F3. CCGT CC comes back in the central, as predicted (§3.5).** 2040: 1.39 GW, 4.8 TWh_e (the 1 Oct central had **0**) with the route *unused*, so the 2040 return comes from the tighter 2040 cap (6.84 Mt, dual −18.9 €/t) and not from the route; 2050: 1.9 TWh_e + 1.0 CHP CC, with the 8 Mt route full. The attribution of each (cap vs route vs biogas cost vs must-runs) would need ablations; none was run. The 1 Oct "solar + storage" central of the Minister's note is therefore no longer reproduced (§7.1). 2050 PV is unchanged at 25.1 GW (13.0 + 12.1 rooftop).
* **F4. 2030 biogas: PyPSA uses the full 8.3 TWh potential, TIMES uses 2.2 TWh.** The potential is by design (master CSV `local_RES_potential`: Valbiom 8.3 TWh in 2025 and 2030, then 6.15 / 4.0 / 5.45 / 6.9 TWh in 2035 / 40 / 45 / 50, the 2040 and 2050 values being the ICEDD–Valbiom cap shared with TIMES). The central runs the whole 8.3 TWh in 2030 (+0.93 TWh forced unsustainable biogas) because at 25.2 €/MWh feedstock it is cheap; réaliste runs only 1.45 TWh (2030 CO₂ cap lifted). The 1 Oct central had the block at the old double-counted 78.8 €/MWh. **Open question for ICEDD:** what 2030 potential TIMES has (its 2.2 TWh is a use, not necessarily a cap) before the 2030 biogas numbers are shown.
* **F5. Réaliste.** 2030 is uncapped (CO₂ dual 0 as designed), so its 2030 objective is 8.2 bn€ lower and its 2040 14.2 bn€ **higher** (inherited investments, the 2040 cap is then tight: dual −24 €/t against −19). 2050 is within 1.1 bn€ of the central. Its heat-pin gap (7.7 TWh) is 3× the others': the 2030 gas-heavy path leaves the 2050 biomass boilers undelivered. It sits on the 30 Sep TIMES export and its 2025 is not row-identical to the central's: any difference against the central mixes TIMES and PyPSA effects. **Not for publication.**
* **F6. Retardnucleaire.** With nuclear pinned at 1 GW_e (3.08 GW_th) in 2045–2050 PyPSA fills the gap with **9.8 TWh_e of CCGT CC (5.3 GW) and 1.9 TWh of CHP CC**, runs **11.5 TWh of FT + methanolisation in Wallonia** (0 in the central) and ends 1.1 bn€ *cheaper* than the central in 2050. The central's 3 GW is a **hard pin** (`agg_p_nom_minmax_demande_haute.csv`, BEWAL `nuclear-all`: min = max = 3 000 MW in 2050, 1 750 in 2040), and retardnucleaire pins 1 000 MW; dropping 2 GW of pinned nuclear saves capex even though the energy then comes from gas + capture. The retardnucleaire `.vd` also differs from the central's, so the cost difference is not purely the pin. This is R4 / R14 of the 30 Sep review again (retardnucleaire 2050 FT in Wallonia), now with the must-runs gone, so it is a genuine optimiser choice. The 8 Mt route is full: the extra capture goes to the route.
* **F7. Biomass-boiler heat is undelivered in 2050 in every scenario.** Pinned 0.5–2.4 TWh_th, undelivered 0.18–2.04 TWh_th, covered by heat pumps. The heat load closes; the *mix* does not transfer. **Cause confirmed: the `biomass limit` global constraint dual is −28 €/MWh in 2040 and about −990 €/MWh in 2050 in all six scenarios** (−988 central, −992 noccsccgt, −992 route, −998 retardnucleaire, −988 biomethane, −987 réaliste): by 2050 the European biomass potential is exhausted, exactly the R9 corner of the checklist (§4.4). **Every 2050 regional biomass number of this batch (solid biomass, biomass boilers, biomass CC, bio-FT) is a degenerate allocation inside a binding aggregate and must not be reported as a regional result.**
* **F8. The CCS decomposition (§1) is not a clean three-way split.** noccsccgt_route − central = **−0.9 bn€ in 2050** (a ban cannot make an LP cheaper unless inputs changed): the NoCCSCCGT `.vd` also changes the soft-link floors (rooftop 13.68 vs 12.14 GW, capture 5.36 vs 5.12 Mt). The *disposal-price* effect (noccsccgt − noccsccgt_route) is clean in structure: **+2.1 bn€** in 2050, route 0 vs 8 Mt, dual −131 €/t. Two caveats for any reading: the ban's own cost cannot be separated from the TIMES delta, and the 2040 effect of the ban is a **BEWAL cap dual of −75 €/t** (against −19), i.e. the pessimistic CCS case really does cost, mostly in 2040.

**Findings**

* **F1. The test works as designed.** The cap removes 7.3 Mt of 2040 export, the Walloon carbon price rises
  several-fold, capture is cut where it is dearest (gas-for-industry and CCGT), and the surplus carbon is
  recycled in Walloon FT and methanolisation. The hypotheses of the 1 Oct design hold, and FT now also appears in 2040.
* **F2. In 2050 the TIMES route and the cap do not conflict, but the route flow no longer means "Walloon CO₂ exported".**
  `BEWAL co2 sequestered` carries 8.0 Mt (the cap of the route), while the net export of lever D is 3.0 Mt:
  Wallonia sends 8.0 Mt down the route and 7.2 Mt out by pipeline, and receives 12.2 Mt by pipeline, a **net import of 5.0 Mt**
  that feeds FT and methanolisation (5.16 Mt). The cap is on the net outflow of the bus, so imports offset it by design
  (the cap's docstring: transit cancels). Read 2050 as "Walloon capture 8.2 Mt − Walloon use 5.2 Mt = 3.0 Mt net export",
  not as "8 Mt exported".
* **F3. The 2050 objective is lower than the central's although the test is more constrained** (−0.79 bn€). The cap
  binds in 2050 but at a low price (dual −7.8 €/t, the carbon goes to FT), so the cause may be the **removed TIMES capture floor** (4.1–5.1 Mt of forced capture in the
  central), not the cap. Hypothesis only: no run with the floor off and the cap off exists. The 1 Oct reading (must-runs) no longer
  applies, since both runs now have them off.
* **F4. 2040 is dearer by 1.07 bn€ (+0.40 %)** for 366 €/t Walloon carbon price: the investment path moves (ground PV
  6.5 vs 4.4 GW, batteries 3.6 vs 1.2 GW, electricity 166 vs 97 €/MWh at BEWAL). The 2040 electricity price effect
  is large and dominated by the binding national cap (−251 €/t), not by the export cap alone.
* **F5. FT at 100 % capex recovery is at the margin**, not a robust finding of "Walloon FT is competitive": a small change
  in oil or H₂ price removes it. Hydrogen is fossil in 2040 (SMR, no electrolysis anywhere): the 2040 FT is gas-to-liquid
  with recycled CO₂, as on 1 Oct, and only the 2050 FT rests on electrolytic H₂ (all abroad, piped in).
* **F6. CCGT CC disappears in 2040 and is halved in 2050**: with exports capped, capturing power-plant CO₂ is no longer
  worthwhile, in contrast to the central's return of power capture (§11 F3).

**Numbers that must not be published as-is:** any Walloon FT or methanolisation capacity as a prediction (F5, 100 % recovery);
the 2050 `BEWAL co2 sequestered` as Walloon exports (F2); 2050 biomass quantities (§11 F7); the 2050 cost comparison with the
central as an effect of the cap (F3).
