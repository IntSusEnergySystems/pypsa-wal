## 3. Foreign nuclear: a corridor from national plans

### 3.1 Why a corridor, not a cap

The 29 Sep log's R1 read France's 49 GW "rebuild" by 2030 as a free investment. **It was
forced.** The caps file carried `legacy-unreviewed` rows in the *min* columns:
* FR ≥ 61 761 MW in 2030 and ≥ 62 907 MW from 2040;
* GB ≥ 5 510 MW in 2030 and ≥ 13 236 MW from 2040.

They bind whenever the `nuclear-2025` options survive (netcal §13.3).

**A floor is still needed.** The model's existing French fleet retires on 38–41-year
lifetimes: 39.4 GW in 2025, 13.1 GW in 2030, 0 in 2050. So "capping" alone would collapse
France.

**What is implemented, per neighbour:**
* **2030–2050: min = max = the national plan**, widened by the row's 1 % tolerance. New
  nuclear is uneconomic at 9 500 €/kW, so the solver sits on whatever floor it is given. A
  floor below the plan would systematically under-build the neighbours.
* **2025: a cap at the real fleet and no floor**, so the base year stays as calibrated.
  The GB, NL and LU caps also let presolve fix three of the four degenerate extendable
  2025 seeds that doubled the 2025 barrier on 29 Sep (netcal §12). France's seed remains:
  the model's fleet is below its 62.9 GW cap.
* **DE and LU: max 0 everywhere.** DE's seed is non-extendable anyway; LU's is extendable
  in `extendable_nuclear_links`.

Rows: `agg:<FR|GB|NL|DE|LU>:nuclear-all:<min|max>` in
`config/input_parameters_for_models.csv`, tag `national-plans-2026-09`. Each row carries
its sources and the unit-by-unit reasoning. The rows are identical in every scenario copy
(`test/test_foreign_nuclear_corridor.py`).

### 3.3 Two things the corridor cannot do (known, accepted)

1. **France cannot go down after 2040.** The `nuclear-2025` link carries its previous
   capacity forward as `p_nom_min`, so capacity can only grow. The chain therefore settles
   at its 2040 level minus the retired old cohorts: **≈ 59.8 GW in 2045 and 2050 instead of
   55 / 54**. That is still inside RTE's N1–N3 range. The cap is clipped up to that floor
   by `add_CCL_constraints`, so nothing becomes infeasible.
2. **The 2025 base year keeps the model's own fleets.** France is 39.4 GW against 62.9
   real. Great Britain is 8.7 GW against 5.9, because Hinkley Point C is dated 2030 in the
   plant data and already runs in 2025. In 2030 the model also carries both HPC units, so
   GB is 4.7 GW against the 2.8 GW plan.

   Fixing France in 2025 means a 2025 floor of ~62.9 GW. That would force ~23.5 GW of
   "new" 2025 nuclear at new-build cost and change the base-year calibration. It is left
   for a separate test (§7).

**Effect to expect against the 29 Sep run:**
* **2030:** FR +1.1 GW, GB −0.8 GW (no forced new build).
* **2040:** FR +3.1 GW, GB −4.1 GW, NL +2.1 GW.
* **2050:** FR ≈ −3 GW, GB +0.8 GW, NL +7 GW.

Belgian import prices move with these, so **no 2030–2050 number of this batch compares
cleanly with the 29 Sep run.**

`cluster/probe.sh` still cannot map Snakemake UUID jobs to scenarios. `nic5.sh status`

```bash

  for s in scen_central $SENS $TEST; do \
```

On each signal:

## 12. Test: less CCS, power-to-fuel back in (`scen_test_lowccs_ptx`)

**Status: designed and wired on 30 Sep; not solved.**
* It runs last tonight (§5.5), at 1 h. A 6 h pre-check was started and then cancelled for
  time.
* It is a **test, not a cabinet scenario:** nothing from it is published.
* If it works, the next step is a TIMES run by ICEDD (§12.6), not a PyPSA deliverable.

### 12.1 The question

Can the central become more ambitious on the energy transition by relying less on
geological CO₂ storage, with the captured carbon recycled into synthetic fuels instead
of buried? What does that cost? [`co2-sequestration.md`](../co2-sequestration.md) sets
the ground rules:
* **A price on storage does not reduce capture.** The binding Walloon cap absorbs it
  about 1:1 (§10.3.1).
* **A quantity works by construction:** a cap on the *net* CO₂ leaving Wallonia (lever D,
  §10.2). Wallonia has no domestic sink.
* **Power-to-fuel is not short of CO₂.** It is held back by the price of hydrogen and, in
  Wallonia, by a must-run artefact (§8.3).

### 12.2 Where power-to-fuel stands in the central

**`co2-sequestration.md` §8.1 ("nothing is built anywhere") is out of date.**
* Lever A, the per-vintage storage ceiling (22 Sep), made storage scarce.
* The 2050 disposal price at BEWAL went from −78 to −348 €/t.

The table reads the 29 Sep 1 h central with `ptx_report.py`. The 30 Sep 6 h smoke test on
tonight's inputs gives the same picture: FT 139 %, electrolysis 83 %, −342 €/t in 2050.

| | 2040 | 2050 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Walloon net CO₂ export (the lever-D metric), Mt/a | 9.33 | 7.97 |
| λ `BEWAL co2 stored`, €/t | −61.5 | −347.8 |
| carbon price seen by a Walloon emitter (system + national dual), €/t | 148 | 677 |
| λ H₂ at BEWAL, €/MWh (what sets it) | 83.2 (unabated SMR) | 128.1 (electrolysis) |
| λ EU oil, €/MWh | 68.6 | 93.4 |
| electrolysis built, GW | 0 | 65 (GB 26, NL 15, DE 13, FR 10); **0 in Belgium** |
| Fischer-Tropsch built, GW of H₂ input | 0 | 17 (GB 8, NL 7, DE 2); **0 in Belgium** |
| BEWAL FT capex recovery: free dispatch / with the 0.5 must-run | 0 % / −61 % | **137 % / 98 %** |
| BEWAL methanolisation, same (0.3 must-run) | 8 % / −20 % | 148 % / 79 % |
| BEWAL electrolysis capex recovery | 23 % | 85 % |

Three readings:
1. **In 2050 the must-run alone keeps FT out of Wallonia.** At free dispatch a Walloon FT
   plant recovers 137 % of its capex. `p_min_pu = 0.5` on an extendable link forces it
   through the negative-margin hours.
2. **In 2040 FT is far away.** At 2040 oil and hydrogen prices and full load, it needs a
   tonne of CO₂ to be worth ≈ 280–290 €/t to it. Disposal pays 61 €/t.
3. **In 2040 hydrogen is fossil.** Unabated SMR sets λ_H₂, so a 2040 FT would run on
   reformed gas. That is gas-to-liquid with recycled CO₂, not power-to-fuel.

### 12.3 The three changes, and why these values

| change | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| **Lever D** (`sector.co2_export_limit`) | BEWAL net export ≤ **3 000 kt/a** in 2040, 2045 and 2050; 2025–2035 free | See **The cap** below |
| **Overage** (`overage_price`) | **500 €/t** on top of the endogenous disposal price | See **The overage** below |
| **TIMES industrial-capture floor** | **off** | The floor (4.1 / 5.1 Mt in 2040 / 2050) transfers TIMES's capture choices, made under TIMES's own 6–7 Mt storage ceiling. Left on, it would sit above the 3 Mt cap and force utilisation or overage by construction. Off, the LP chooses: recycle, capture less and abate elsewhere, or pay |
| **Power-to-X must-runs** | FT 0.5 → **0**; methanolisation 0.3 → **0**; methanation 0.3 → **0** | A must-run on an extendable link, with no unit commitment anywhere in the network, is an LP artefact (§8.3). Agreed on 30 Sep: "fine to remove the 50 % minimum load". These are prepare-time keys, so the test has its own prepared trees |

**The cap.** The value is arbitrary by design: the request was to limit CCS
arbitrarily. It was chosen to be:
* **round:** half of TIMES-WAL's own `CO2STG01` ceiling (6 Mt/a in 2040), and a third of
  what the PyPSA central exports;
* **below the ~4.7–5.0 Mt/a of process-emission capture.** Not even the unavoidable
  cement and lime CO₂ can all be stored, so the test forces the question of where it goes.

2030 stays free, so the chain matches the central up to 2030 and the effect is isolated
in 2040–2050. A 2030 cap is a separate question (§7 item 10).

**The overage.** A hard cap can make the LP infeasible, so the valve is mandatory (§10.2).
500 €/t only prevents infeasibility; it does not replace the cap:
* it is above the 2040 FT threshold (~280–290 €/t);
* added to the disposal price, it is above what emitting costs in 2040 (148 €/t).

A positive overage means the cap is too tight at that price, which is a result in itself.

**Everything else is unchanged:**
* the central's `.vd`, the rooftop floor and the import cap;
* the national and system CO₂ caps;
* all costs and potentials;
* `dac: false`.

`test/test_lowccs_ptx_scenario.py` pins "the central plus exactly these three".

**The accounting is the central's.**
* The national cap reads only flows into `co2 atmosphere`. So CO₂ captured and then used
  counts as not emitted in Wallonia.
* The synthetic fuel's combustion is booked where it is burnt. That is mostly aviation,
  which is outside the national caps (co2-sequestration.md §9.1).

### 12.4 What to expect (hypotheses, to check against the solve)

* **2025–2030:** as the central. The only difference is the dispatch of the few existing
  methanolisation units, now without their must-run.
* **2050: the cap removes ~5 Mt/a of export.**
  * Emitting instead would cost ~677 €/t, so the tonnes stay captured and become synthetic
    fuel in Wallonia. FT is above break-even once the must-run is gone.
  * Order of magnitude: 5 Mt/a at 0.194 tCO₂/MWh_H₂ is ~26 TWh/a of hydrogen, ~19 TWh/a of
    synthetic oil and ~3.5–4 GW_H₂ of FT.
  * The hydrogen comes mostly through the pipeline network from electrolysis abroad. In
    the 2050 central, hydrogen is electrolytic everywhere. Some may come from Walloon
    electrolysis, if it clears its 85 %.
* **2040 is the real test: the cap removes ~6.3 Mt/a.** The LP has three ways out:
  * capture less (CCGT CC, gas-for-industry CC, BECCS) and abate elsewhere under the
    national cap;
  * make synthetic fuel, once a tonne is worth ~280–290 €/t to it;
  * pay the overage.

  Expect the Walloon carbon price to rise several-fold. If FT appears, check its
  hydrogen: in 2040 it is likely SMR-based, i.e. not power-to-fuel.
* **Every horizon: the objective rises.** The system cap is unchanged, so the rise is the
  price of avoiding storage. Under the "buried or recycled" identity (co2-sequestration.md
  §9.4), system emissions do not fall: the carbon is recycled instead of buried, and
  fossil oil imports fall.

### 12.7 If it does not work

* **No Walloon PtX even in 2050.** Something other than the economics of §12.2 binds.
  Candidates: the H₂ pipeline capacity into BEWAL, the heat sink for FT waste heat, or the
  national cap going slack. Read the duals before changing anything.
* **In 2040, power-to-fuel only on SMR hydrogen.** This is the expected weak point. The
  levers of the right size are electrolyser capex (−50 to −70 % needed at 2040) and a
  synthetic-fuel mandate (co2-sequestration.md §10.1). More CCS limits are not.
* **DAC, as a last resort** (agreed 30 Sep):
  * **How:** copy the block to `scen_test_lowccs_ptx_dac` with `sector: dac: true`. It is
    a prepare-time key, so the copy needs its own prepare.
  * **Not needed for feedstock:** the cap leaves CO₂ in Wallonia in abundance.
  * **A different scenario, not a variant:** DAC would be built wherever the system CO₂
    price (659 €/t in 2050) exceeds its cost, which changes the whole system
    (co2-sequestration.md §9.5 d).
