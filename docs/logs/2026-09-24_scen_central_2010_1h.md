## 9. Issues encountered and fixes

1. **Root disk filled during the first prepare.** `scripts/build_ship_raster.py` extracted an 8.6 GB `shipdensity_global.tif` next to the Snakemake output, which lived on `/`. Root went from 82 % to 100 % (256 KB free). `build_shapes` then failed with `OSError: [Errno 28]`. Home (`/home`, already 85 % full) was not written. The tif was moved onto `/sylvain/mount`, and the heavy trees that had landed on root (`resources/walloon_5y`, `results/walloon_5y`, `results/times-pypsa`, `results/walloon-model`, `results/_heat_softlink_comparison`) were moved there too. `resources/` and `results/` themselves stay real git directories; only the children are symlinks onto the mount (a parent symlink makes git drop `.gitkeep`). The extractor now writes to `TMPDIR`, or to `/sylvain/mount/pypsa-wal-data/tmp` when that parent exists. The leftover tif on the mount was deleted.
2. **2025 national CO₂ ratio must stay a hole.** A blank active year was being interpolated back to the 2030 anchor (0.45). `build_budget_national` now drops an explicit blank year, and `solve_network.py` skips `add_co2limit_country` when that year is absent. The solved 2025 network has `CO2Limit` and no `co2_limit_per_countryBEWAL`. Confirmed below.
3. **Sequestration rows from `development_plan` were not applied**, on purpose. They are `pending` with target `none:placeholder_to_refine`, the VOM is not in EUR2025, and a year-dependent sequestration cost was already rejected in `docs/co2-sequestration.md`. The currency check now ignores `none:` targets so `--write` is not blocked by them.
4. **`build_common_parameters.py` under the base Python** failed (`to_csv` has no `lineterminator` on the old pandas). The writer was run in the `pypsa-eur` env. `--check` passed. Technology-data drift warnings (Fischer-Tropsch, HVDC FOM, nuclear fuel) are pre-existing notes.
5. **Cluster login node printed “pypsa2html not installed”.** The HTML was built on the workstation and published from there.

### Findings

Ranked by whether they change a number someone would quote.

**R1 — 2050 Walloon nuclear stays at the lifetime-extension floor. The previous central run built the cap.**

2050 BEWAL nuclear is 1 030 MW_e against a corridor maximum of 3 000 MW_e (extendable tranche 1 030, corridor ceiling 3 015). BEVLG is 1 000 MW_e against a maximum of 3 000. The same pair is already 1 030 / 1 000 in 2030 and 2040. In 2025 the inherited fleet is larger: BEWAL 1 992 MW_e, BEVLG 1 890 MW_e.

The 13 September central run built BEWAL nuclear to 3 000 MW_e in 2050. This run fills the gap with gas: 2050 BEWAL CCGT 2 626 MW_e plus CCGT with capture 3 416 MW_e, together 6 042 MW_e, against a combined 1 740 MW_e in 2025 and 2030.

The corridor is a maximum, so 1 030 MW_e is a legal optimum, not a constraint bug. It is the 2050 power-mix headline, and it should be stated as “the model did not build the new nuclear the cap allows”, not as “nuclear is capped at 1 030”.

**R2 — the BEWAL CO₂ price is much tighter than in the 13 September central run, and 2025 is not the same price concept.**

| Year | system dual (EUR/t) | national dual (EUR/t) | effective | 13 Sep effective |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2025 | 83.6 | none | 84 | 456 |
| 2030 | 186.7 | 244.3 | 431 | 186 |
| 2040 | 162.0 | 19.5 | 181 | 141 |
| 2050 | 644.0 | 117.5 | 761 | 478 |

The missing 2025 national dual is the blank emission-ratio row, and it is what was asked for. The system cap still binds (dual 83.6 EUR/t on a constant of 1.471e9). Quoting a single “2025 Belgian carbon price” from this run would describe only the European budget.

2030 and 2050 are the years where the price itself is the headline. 2030 national dual 244 EUR/t is larger than the system dual. 2050 at 761 EUR/t is consistent with a slack sequestration limit (R5) and with gas-with-capture carrying the energy the nuclear cap was not asked to fill (R1).

**R3 — the Walloon PV mix is not comparable to the 13 September run.**

Tracking PV is gone (0 MW). Ground PV in 2050 is 10 601 MW, where the previous run built 0. Rooftop in 2050 is 18 865 MW, where the previous run built 9 012. Combined PV in 2050 is about 29.5 GW against about 12.8 GW before. Onshore wind is unchanged at the 6 500 MW potential from 2040.

2025 still matches the old stock (ground 911 + rooftop 1 770 = 2 681 MW, the same total as ground + tracking + rooftop on 13 September). The divergence starts at the first investment horizon, which is what a floor-instead-of-share plus the removal of tracking should do.

2050 rooftop capacity factor is 10.8 % (17.81 TWh / 18 865 MW), inside the 7–14 % band. The volume is large; the yield per megawatt is ordinary.

**R7 — 2025 heat-pump COP is 2.32, under the checklist’s 2.5–3.5 band.**

Effective COP is heat delivered over electricity consumed: 2025 is 1.27 TWh_th / 0.55 TWh_e = 2.32. Later years are 2.52, 2.45 and 2.57, inside the band. The review script marked 2025 as ok against its own interval; the written checklist still says 2.5–3.5. 2025 heat-pump capacity is the inherited stock (1 201 MW), and the profile check closes that stock to the TIMES shape (worst peak gap 0.0008 MW). The low COP is a property of the 2025 stock and the 2010 weather, not a failed soft-link.

BEWAL hydrogen throughput is still real: the H2 bus balances at 0.0 / 20.8 / 13.0 / 19.0 TWh (2025 residual is the numerical warning below).
