\begin{abstract}
\noindent
PyPSA-Wal caps Walloon onshore wind at \CapToday{}~MW. That number is an expert
judgement taken from the PNEC wallon and from EDORA; it is not derived from a
land calculation, and the model's own calculation --- the one PyPSA-Eur performs
with \texttt{atlite} --- was never run against Walloon law. This report closes
that gap. It computes the potential from the Walloon Region's own regulatory
instruments --- the plan de secteur and the Code du Développement Territorial,
the \emph{cadre de référence éolien} of 25~January~2024, the Natura~2000
network, the nature reserves, the landscape inventories, the natural-hazard
maps, the aeronautical obstacle-evaluation map and the classified-site
registers, all retrieved live from the Géoportail de la Wallonie. The
constraints are calibrated against the \FleetN{} turbines standing in Wallonia,
and turned into capacity by placing machines and parks under explicit rules
rather than by multiplying area by a density.

\AdminEligible{}~\si{\square\kilo\metre} of the Walloon Region survive every
exclusion, \AdminEligibleShare{}\,\% of the territory. Packing machines on that
land at \PlaceRotorD{}~rotor diameters apart, with no rule about how they group,
gives \FreePnom{}~MW: a land-and-wake bound. Requiring parks of at least
\PlaceMinTurbines{} machines gives \NoHorizonPnom{}~MW, and applying the
landscape rule the framework states as a result --- \PlaceAzimuth{}° of every
village's horizon, within \PlaceHorizonRadius{}~km, free of turbines --- gives
the gross reference of \textbf{\PlacePnom{}~MW} in \PlaceParks{} parks. A
documented allowance for the two constraint families that still have no public
geometry brings the central estimate to \textbf{\CredibleCentralGW{}~GW}, in a
credible range of \CredibleLowGW{}--\CredibleHighGW{}~GW.

Four results matter more than the headline number.

\textbf{The answer is decided by policy rules, and each is priced.} The Cadre is
a circular ``à valeur indicative'' and the plan de secteur admits derogations
for wind farms, so most of the rules that shape the answer are policy choices
a permit may depart from. Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity} prices every one of them
on the reference: admitting agricultural land away from the main infrastructure
network (the derogation route of CoDT art.~D.IV.11) is worth
\SensNoCorridorPct{}\,\%, dropping the minimum park size \SensParksOnePct{}\,\%,
and applying the 2013 habitat setback \SensHabitatOldPct{}\,\%.

\textbf{The 4--6~km inter-distance is in the 2024 framework, as a
recommendation.} It is recommended between parks, ``peut être réduite selon la
situation paysagère locale'', does not apply along motorways, and is measured
between the nearest masts. \FleetEdgeBelowFour{}\,\% of the standing farms have
a neighbour closer than 4~km. Applied as a hard rule it would bring the gross
reference to \IdFourMW{}~MW (4~km) or \IdSixMW{}~MW (6~km); it is therefore
reported as a sensitivity, not built into the reference.

\textbf{BREGILAB's \BregTotalGW{}~GW is a land-and-wake bound, and it cannot be
reproduced from its own documentation.} Its allocation packs machines on
isolated pixels and applies neither the park nor the landscape rules of the
Cadre; on this study's land those two rules alone divide the land-and-wake bound
by \FreeOverRef{}. Its documented constraint set, applied to open Walloon
data, gives \BregEmuGW{}~GW; its published figure is \BregReportedShare{}\,\% of
that. That its headline sits close to this study's land-and-wake bound is a
coincidence of a looser documented rule set and a tighter undocumented one.

\textbf{PyPSA-Eur's default land analysis is not usable here.} It returns
\BasePnom{}~MW for the Walloon Region, because it admits broad-leaved forest,
ignores the plan de secteur entirely and cannot see scattered housing.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question, and the scope of the answer}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{The number under review}

The Walloon onshore-wind capacity limit in PyPSA-Wal is a single row of
\texttt{config/input\_parameters\_for\_models.csv}:

\begin{quote}\small
\texttt{potential:BEWAL:onwind:p\_nom\_max} $=$ \CapToday{}~MW, source
\emph{``PNEC Wallon et EDORA''}, described as \emph{``potentiel technique,
calculé comme la somme de ce qui est plausible d'atteindre en 2030 comme
capacité et des projets à l'étude''}, with a note that EDORA estimates an
additional 3.8~GW of wind potential by 2050 on the basis of projects under
environmental-impact assessment.
\end{quote}

Two things follow from that wording. It is a \emph{deployment} expectation
assembled from a pipeline of known projects, not a \emph{land} potential; and it
carries no spatial calculation that anyone can re-run or contest. For an
internal scenario exercise that is acceptable. For a number reported to the
administration as the ceiling on what Wallonia can host it is not: the first
question is \emph{``where does \CapTodayGW{}~GW fit, and under which rules?''},
and the current answer is a literature reference.

\subsection{What PyPSA-Eur would have said}

PyPSA-Eur ships exactly the tool needed --- \texttt{atlite}'s land-eligibility
analysis, driven by \texttt{scripts/determine\_availability\_matrix.py}. It is
run for every other node in the model. For the Walloon node its output is
overridden by the CSV row above, so its answer has never been examined. It is
\BasePnom{}~MW (\BasePnomGW{}~GW) for the Walloon Region, on
\BaseEligible{}~\si{\square\kilo\metre} of admitted land.

That the override exists is defensible --- Section~\ref{sec:pypsaeur} shows the
default constraint set is unusable in Wallonia. That the override was never
compared against a corrected calculation is the gap this report fills.

\subsection{What this report answers, and what it does not}

This is a land-eligibility \emph{and siting} study. It answers: how much Walloon
land may legally and physically host a wind turbine, and how many machines can
stand on that land once the framework's rules about grouping and landscape are
applied? It does not answer: how much of that capacity will be permitted,
financed and built, or in what year. A technical potential is an upper bound on
the model's search space, and that is precisely the role \texttt{p\_nom\_max}
plays in PyPSA-Wal --- the build-rate limit and the 2030 corridor, documented in
\texttt{docs/renewable-potentials.md}, carry the deployment question.

The Walloon rules are of two kinds. Some decide whether a mast may stand at a
given point at all --- the zoning, the setbacks, the protected areas. Others
decide how machines may be arranged --- in parks, away from the horizons of
villages, at a distance from other parks. The second kind is largely landscape
policy, much of it indicative, and the answer depends on it more than on the
first. This report therefore builds a reference on the rules as they are stated
and applied, and prices every rule that is a policy or a modelling choice
separately (Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}), so that a reader who would decide
one of them differently can read off the consequence.

\subsection{Which Wallonia}\label{sec:region}

Everything here is computed for the \textbf{administrative Walloon Region}, the
union of the 21 \texttt{BE3} NUTS-3 areas, \adminArea{}~\si{\square\kilo\metre}.
That is the territory the Walloon rules apply to and the territory an
administration will ask about.

It is not the same polygon as the model's \texttt{BEWAL} node. PyPSA-Eur builds
onshore regions by Voronoi-partitioning each country around its substations and
clipping to the country shape; the partition is electrical, not administrative.
The \texttt{BEWAL} polygon covers \modelArea{}~\si{\square\kilo\metre}, or
\modelCoverage{}\,\% of the Region: \areaOutsideModel{}~\si{\square\kilo\metre}
of Walloon territory --- essentially the whole of western Hainaut, from Tournai
and Mouscron to Ath --- sits on the Flemish side of the partition, while
\areaOutsideWallonia{}~\si{\square\kilo\metre} of the \texttt{BEWAL} polygon lies
outside Wallonia, mostly over the French border.

\caveat{A potential computed on one of those two regions and applied to the other
is wrong regardless of how much it moves the number, and the defect affects every
land-based Walloon quantity in PyPSA-Wal --- ground-mounted solar and biomass
among them. Fixing it means correcting the busmap, which is out of scope here and
should be raised separately. Until it is, a figure from this report applied to
\texttt{BEWAL} grants the node the resource of land the model has already given
to Flanders.}

\section{Method}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{The workflow}

The analysis lives in \texttt{workflow\_onwind\_wal/}, a self-contained
Snakemake workflow. It shares nothing with the main PyPSA-Wal workflow except
two read-only inputs: the region shapes and the \texttt{atlite} ERA5 cutout. It
writes only inside its own directory and into
\texttt{docs/onwind\_potential\_wallonia/}. Running it, breaking it or deleting
its outputs cannot affect a model run.

\begin{verbatim}
  cd workflow_onwind_wal
  snakemake -c4 download   # ~20 min, one-off, Walloon and OSM open data
  snakemake -c4 all        # eligibility, placement, sensitivity, figures
  snakemake -c4 report     # this PDF
\end{verbatim}

The stages are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Retrieve.} Two dozen reference datasets are pulled from the
    ESRI-REST services of \url{https://geoservices.wallonie.be} (CC-BY~4.0),
    paginated, and written as GeoPackages with a provenance sidecar recording
    the service, the layer, the feature count and the retrieval date
    (Annex~\ref{sec:sources}). Feature counts are checked against the counts
    observed on 22~September~2026 and a deviation above 20\,\% raises a warning:
    if the Region revises a dataset, the workflow says so rather than silently
    changing the answer. Three inputs come from elsewhere and have their own
    retrieval scripts: the slope-class grid, decoded from a visualisation
    service (§\ref{sec:newlayers}); the standing fleet, and the dual
    carriageways and high-speed railway lines, from OpenStreetMap
    (§\ref{sec:corridor}, §\ref{sec:calibration}).
  \item \textbf{Exclusion layers.} The regulatory rules are translated into
    geometry, once per turbine class, because most setbacks scale with tip
    height or rotor diameter (Section~\ref{sec:rules}). Every plan-de-secteur
    label the configuration names is checked against the data, so a spelling
    variant cannot silently drop a zone.
  \item \textbf{Eligibility.} \texttt{atlite}'s \texttt{ExclusionContainer}
    burns every layer onto a \SI{100}{\metre} raster in EPSG:3035 and
    \texttt{cutout.availabilitymatrix} aggregates the surviving share per ERA5
    cell --- the identical code path PyPSA-Eur uses, so the two results are
    comparable by construction.
  \item \textbf{Calibration.} The constraint set and the siting rules are tested
    against the machines that are actually standing (§\ref{sec:calibration}).
  \item \textbf{Placement.} Machines, then parks, are allocated on the eligible
    raster (§\ref{sec:placement}). Every layout is checked against its own rules
    by an independent implementation, and the run fails if one is violated.
  \item \textbf{Sensitivity.} Every policy and modelling choice is priced on its
    own against the reference (§\ref{sec:sensitivity}), and BREGILAB's
    potential is rebuilt from its own rules on the same data
    (§\ref{sec:bregilab}).
  \item \textbf{Report.} Tables, figures and every number in this text are
    generated. Nothing here is typed by hand.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why the constraints are declared in the config, not in the code}

Every exclusion is a block of \texttt{workflow\_onwind\_wal/config.yaml} naming
the dataset, the legal instrument and the buffer, and every sensitivity case is
a block naming the one rule it changes. Reviewing the constraint set --- which
is what the administration will want to do --- requires reading one YAML file,
not the Python. Changing a rule is a config edit, and Snakemake re-derives only
what depends on it.

\subsection{From eligible land to capacity: machines and parks are placed}
\label{sec:placement}

The conventional step from eligible area to installable capacity is a
multiplication by a capacity density in MW/\si{\square\kilo\metre}. That
assumes the land arrives in blocks large enough to lay out an array. Walloon
eligible land does not: it arrives as \NPatches{} disconnected patches with a
median size of \MedianPatch{}~ha and a largest of
\LargestPatch{}~\si{\square\kilo\metre}, against
\Footprint{}~\si{\square\kilo\metre} for one cell of a $5D\times7D$ array. An
area-times-density figure therefore counts land no machine could occupy. The
obvious repair --- screening the patches by size --- fails in the other
direction, because a real turbine does not have to sit inside one contiguous
eligible polygon: it may stand at the edge of its patch with its rotor
overhanging ineligible land, and its access track and cabling cross ineligible
land as a matter of course. That is legally correct here, because every Walloon
setback is measured from the \emph{mast}.

This study therefore allocates machines explicitly, under four rules. Each is
justified separately below, each is tested against the \FleetN{} machines
standing in Wallonia (§\ref{sec:calibration}), and the last is not part of the
reference.

\subsubsection{Rule 1 --- how close two machines may stand}

A machine is accepted wherever no machine already placed, in any park, lies
closer than a minimum inter-turbine distance. That distance is a wake
criterion, and three readings of it are carried:

\begin{table}[htbp]\centering\small
\caption{Minimum inter-turbine distance, in rotor diameters, and what it gives
on the reference land: before any grouping rule, and in the reference model.}
\label{tab:spacing}
\begin{tabular}{lp{0.42\linewidth}rrr}
\toprule
case & basis & $d/D$ & free [\si{\mega\watt}] & reference [\si{\mega\watt}] \\
\midrule
\emph{observed} & median nearest neighbour of the standing Walloon fleet
  & \SpObsD{} & \SpObsFree{} & \SpObsMW{} \\
\emph{crosswind} & nearest-neighbour distance of a $5D\times7D$ array; the rule
  BREGILAB uses & \SpCrossD{} & \SpCrossFree{} & \SpCrossMW{} \\
\emph{isotropic} & $\sqrt{5\times7}\,D$, the area-equivalent of that array
  & \SpIsoD{} & \SpIsoFree{} & \SpIsoMW{} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{The reference is \SpCrossD{}\,$D$.} Converting an area-per-turbine into
an isotropic \emph{minimum separation} forbids exactly the pairs the convention
it comes from contains: in a $5D\times7D$ array the closest two machines are
$5D$ apart, not $\sqrt{35}\,D$. It also assumes a two-dimensional array, whereas
Walloon wind farms are predominantly strings along a road, a ridge or a field
boundary, where there is no downwind row and the $7D$ never binds. The physical
floor is lower still --- about $3D$, below which wake-induced turbulence takes a
machine outside the standard IEC~61400-1 load case --- and the standing Walloon
fleet sits at a median of \FleetMachineNNPFifty{}~m between neighbours, about
\SpObsD{}\,$D$ at the \SI{100}{\metre}-class rotor of that fleet, with a quarter
of machines closer than \FleetMachineNNPTwentyFive{}~m. \SpCrossD{}\,$D$ is
therefore already the conservative end of practice.

The free allocation this rule produces on its own is the \emph{land-and-wake
bound}: \FreePnom{}~MW, \FreeTurbines{} machines, an effective
\FreeDensity{}~MW/\si{\square\kilo\metre} of eligible land. That density is far
above any array density because the wake spacing between two machines on
different patches is satisfied by the ineligible land between them. It depends
on the order in which the land is walked --- a pseudo-random order gives a
result \FreeOrderSpread{}\,\% below the row-major one --- and it puts a
near-continuous band of machines through every corridor in the Region. It is
the quantity BREGILAB reports (§\ref{sec:bregilab}), and it is not a technical
potential under the Walloon rules, because those rules are about how machines
are arranged as much as about where a mast may stand.

\subsubsection{Rule 2 --- machines come in parks}

The framework in force defines a park as ``un projet éolien qui prévoit
l'implantation de minimum quatre éoliennes'' (Cadre~2024, §3.1). Parks are
therefore grown from seed cells, ranked by how much eligible land surrounds
them, machine by machine and nearest-first. A machine within
\PlaceLink{}~km of a park machine belongs to that park --- the single-linkage
distance used to group the standing fleet into farms --- and a park is kept only
if it reaches \PlaceMinTurbines{} machines.

Nothing else constrains the layout. There is deliberately no farm radius and no
separation between farm centres: those are geometry, not rules. A model built on
non-overlapping discs sterilises the land between the discs --- a greedy packing
of discs covers little more than half of the plane --- and on this study's land a
model of farm discs \SI{1.3}{\kilo\metre} in radius and \SI{2.6}{\kilo\metre}
apart gives about as little as the park model under the strictest reading of the
inter-distance, 6~km, without any text to justify it.

The minimum of four is itself a policy choice. The same article allows it to be
reduced for repowering, for an extension of machines in operation, in a zone
d'activité économique for machines above \SI{3.2}{\mega\watt}, and for any
machine above \SI{3.2}{\mega\watt} ``pour autant qu'il s'intègre harmonieusement
dans le paysage et ne réduise pas le potentiel éolien de la zone'' --- which the
reference class is. It is applied in the reference because it describes how
Walloon wind is actually built: \FleetInBigFarms{}\,\% of the standing machines
are in groups of four or more. Without it the reference would be
\ParksOnePnom{}~MW (§\ref{sec:sensitivity}).

\subsubsection{Rule 3 --- every village keeps an open horizon}

The framework states the landscape criterion as a result to be preserved
(Cadre~2024, §3.4~§3, which repeats the 2013 text):

\begin{quote}\small\itshape
Pour éviter les situations d'encerclement des villages, un angle horizontal de
130° sans éoliennes est préservé sur une distance de 4 kilomètres.
\end{quote}

It is implemented as written. Every village --- a contiguous block of
plan-de-secteur habitat zoning above \SI{10}{\hectare}, \HorizonSettlements{} of
them --- carries the arcs its surrounding machines occupy, each machine
subtending the angle its rotor covers from the village centre. A machine is
refused if admitting it would take any village's largest turbine-free arc below
\PlaceAzimuth{}° within \PlaceHorizonRadius{}~km. The standing fleet respects
the rule almost everywhere (§\ref{sec:horizoncheck}), which is why it belongs in
the reference.

\subsubsection{Rule 4 --- the inter-distance between parks, as a sensitivity}

The same paragraph continues:

\begin{quote}\small\itshape
Sauf lorsque les éoliennes sont implantées le long des autoroutes, une
interdistance minimale de 4~km (en zone de paysage à vues courtes) à 6~km (en
zone de paysage à vues longues) est recommandée et peut être réduite selon la
situation paysagère locale.
\end{quote}

Five things in that sentence and in practice decide how it is treated.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item It is in the framework in force. The 2024 circular carries it over from
    the 2013 framework, which called it a ``référence indicative'' to be taken
    into consideration in the light of the impact assessment; the 2024 text
    keeps the figures and makes the status explicit: \emph{recommandée}, and
    reducible according to the local landscape.
  \item It does not apply along motorways, and it depends on the landscape:
    4~km where views are short, 6~km where they are long.
  \item It is measured between the nearest masts of two parks. The Pôle
    Environnement's 2024 opinion on a project at Chimay, for instance, finds the
    4~km of the short-view Fagne-Famenne ``non respectée'' at 785~m, 1.2~km and
    1.8~km from three neighbouring parks, among the reasons for an
    unfavourable opinion on a project whose permit was refused. The rule is
    invoked in permitting.
  \item It is not what the standing fleet looks like. Measured between nearest
    masts, \FleetEdgeBelowFour{}\,\% of the \FleetFarms{} standing farms have a
    neighbour closer than 4~km and \FleetEdgeBelowSix{}\,\% one closer than
    6~km; leaving out the \FleetMwFarms{} farms that stand along a motorway,
    \FleetNonMwEdgeBelowFour{}\,\% are still closer than 4~km
    (§\ref{sec:fleetgeometry}). The reduction the text allows is the rule in
    practice rather than the exception.
  \item The whole circular is indicative: ``Un permis éolien peut s'écarter du
    Cadre, moyennant une motivation formelle adéquate'' (§1.4).
\end{enumerate}

It is implemented exactly as stated and priced as a sensitivity: a machine is
refused if it would stand closer than 4 or 6~km to a machine of another park,
unless both stand within \MotorwayExempt{}~km of a motorway. A single uniform
figure overstates the rule, since the text asks for 4~km only where views are
short and allows both to be reduced; the two cases bracket it.

\finding{The land-and-wake bound is \FreePnom{}~MW. Grouping machines into parks
of \PlaceMinTurbines{} or more costs \ParksCostPct{}\,\% of it and gives
\NoHorizonPnom{}~MW; the open-horizon rule costs a further
\HorizonCostPct{}\,\% and gives the gross reference of \PlacePnom{}~MW in
\PlaceParks{} parks of \PlacePerPark{} machines on average. The recommended
inter-distance, applied on top as a hard rule, would give \IdFourMW{}~MW at
4~km and \IdSixMW{}~MW at 6~km. An onshore-wind potential quoted without saying
which of these rules it carries cannot be compared with anything.}

\section{The Walloon regulatory framework}\label{sec:rules}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Sources of law and of practice}

Five instruments define where a wind turbine may stand in Wallonia:

\begin{description}
  \item[CoDT] the \emph{Code du Développement Territorial}, which fixes the
    zoning categories of the \emph{plan de secteur}. It admits wind turbines in
    agricultural zones whose mast is within \SI{1500}{\metre} of the axis of a
    \emph{principale infrastructure de communication} (PIC) or of the limit of a
    zone d'activité économique (art.~D.II.36~§2, R.II.36-2), and in forest zones
    whose mast is within \SI{750}{\metre} of a PIC, outside a recognised
    conservation site and outside a broad-leaved stand (D.II.37~§1, R.II.37-2).
    A PIC is defined at art.~R.II.21-1: ``les autoroutes et les routes de
    liaisons régionales à deux fois deux bandes de circulation'', the railway
    lines other than purely touristic ones, and the navigable waterways. Two
    further provisions matter. A \emph{périmètre d'intérêt paysager} is not an
    exclusion: works there ``peuvent y être autorisés pour autant qu'ils
    contribuent à la protection, à la gestion ou à l'aménagement du paysage''
    (R.II.21-7). And the permit for a wind farm, an act of public interest
    delivered by the fonctionnaire délégué (D.IV.22 al.~1 7°~k), ``peut être
    accordé en dérogeant au plan de secteur'' (D.IV.11), under the conditions of
    D.IV.13.
  \item[Cadre de référence éolien] the reference framework for wind development,
    adopted as a government circular on 25~January~2024 and applicable to
    applications filed from 25~April~2024; it replaced the 2013 framework. It is
    ``une circulaire du gouvernement à valeur indicative'' (§1.4). It excludes
    turbines from habitat, rural habitat, habitat-vert and residential-ZACC
    zones, from natural and park zones and from leisure zones with housing, and
    admits them in forest only on coniferous plantations of low biological
    value (§3.2~§1). It sets the mast at least \textbf{\SI{500}{\metre} plus
    half the total height} from those residential zones and \textbf{\SI{400}{\metre}
    from any dwelling}, except operators' housing in a zone d'activité
    économique (§3.2~§2), where the 2013 framework asked for four times the
    total height from habitat zones. It defines a park as at least four

\subsection{The zoning rule that dominates: the agricultural corridor}
\label{sec:corridor}

The CoDT does not treat agricultural land as uniformly available. It admits a
turbine there only within \SI{1.5}{\kilo\metre} of the axis of a PIC or of the
limit of a zone d'activité économique, and in coniferous forest only within
\SI{750}{\metre} of a PIC. This is a \emph{concentration} rule --- keep wind
development along the existing structuring infrastructure rather than scattered
across the open landscape --- and it has no analogue in PyPSA-Eur, whose own
treatment works the other way, by \emph{excluding} a \SI{1}{\kilo\metre} band
around transport infrastructure.

What counts as a PIC decides how much land the rule admits. The plan-de-secteur
road layer is not the PIC network: besides \PicMotorwayKm{}~km of motorways it
draws some \PdsRoadKm{}~km of roads in total, most of them two-lane
\emph{routes de liaison}. Taking every one of them as a PIC puts a
\SI{1.5}{\kilo\metre} corridor around almost every main road of the Region and
admits a large share of the agricultural land the rule is meant to keep free. The road part of the network is built here from
the plan-de-secteur motorways and the OpenStreetMap dual carriageways ---
motorways, and trunk, primary and secondary roads drawn as one-way pairs with a
limit of \SI{70}{\kilo\metre\per\hour} or more, \PicDualKm{}~km of carriageway
--- which is what ``deux fois deux bandes de circulation'' means on the ground.
Reading every plan-de-secteur road as a PIC instead would move the
reference by \SensPicPdsPct{}\,\% (§\ref{sec:sensitivity}).

The corridor governs a permit \emph{in conformity} with the plan de secteur. A
wind farm can also be permitted in derogation of it, and that route is used:
\FleetZoningCodt{}\,\% of the standing machines stand on land the zoning admits
with the CoDT's PIC network, \FleetZoningPsroads{}\,\% with every road taken as
a PIC. The derogation route --- agricultural land admitted wherever the other
rules allow --- is the largest single policy lever in this study and is priced in
§\ref{sec:sensitivity}.

\subsection{Setbacks for the three turbine classes}

The turbine classes mirror the three \emph{scénarios de base} of the 2022
favourable-zone update (total tip height 150, 180 and 210~m) so the two studies
can be read side by side. The reference class is \refTurbine{}: rotor
\RotorDiameter{}~m, tip height \TipHeight{}~m, giving a habitat-zone setback of
\HabitatSetback{}~m under the 2024 framework.

\caveat{Read as $4H$ on the total height --- the rule of the 2013 framework ---
the habitat setback would be \SI{740}{\metre} for the reference class instead of
\HabitatSetback{}~m. The 2024 rule is used because it is the one in force; the
2013 rule is priced in §\ref{sec:sensitivity} and available in the config
(\texttt{setbacks.habitat\_zone.default: cdr2013}).}

\subsection{The three constraint families added from data that had to be
rebuilt}\label{sec:newlayers}

Three of the families in Table~\ref{tab:rules} are not published as ready-made
exclusion polygons. They are reconstructed here, and each reconstruction is a
hypothesis in its own right.

\paragraph{Aeronautical servitudes.} The Region publishes
\emph{Hauteurs pouvant impacter l'aviation} (AOEM), which gives, for every point
of Wallonia, the height above which the Direction générale Transport Aérien must
be consulted. The six classes --- 0, 15, 25, 35, 50 and 60~m --- tile the
territory exactly. Four of them are nested rings around the aerodromes (Liège,
Charleroi, Beauvechain, Florennes, Chièvres, Saint-Hubert, Bertrix, Spa and the
smaller fields); two are the territory-wide notification background, one polygon
of \SI{8045}{\square\kilo\metre} at 25~m and one of
\SI{1556}{\square\kilo\metre} at 60~m. Any wind turbine exceeds every one of
those thresholds, so the map is a consultation duty and not, on its face, an
exclusion. Which of the aerodrome rings is a genuine no-go is settled
empirically in §\ref{sec:calibration}: the 0~m and 15~m rings are strongly
avoided by the standing fleet and are excluded here; the 35~m and 50~m rings
carry \emph{more} turbines per square kilometre than Wallonia as a whole and are
not.

\paragraph{The \SI{7}{\percent} slope criterion.} The Walloon favourable-zone
methodology treats slopes above \SI{7}{\percent} as incompatible with the crane
pad and access track of a large machine. It is a technical criterion of that
methodology, not a rule of the CoDT or of the Cadre. The Region publishes the
slope grid --- \texttt{ERRUISOL\_CLASSE\_PENTE}, per cent slope derived from the
\SI{10}{\metre} ERRUISSOL terrain model --- but only as a visualisation service,
with no feature query and no coverage download. What the service does expose is
an image export that renders an exact bounding box into an exact number of
pixels with nearest-neighbour resampling and a seven-colour palette, one colour
per slope class. Decoding those colours recovers the class grid losslessly, and
the \SI{7}{\percent} threshold falls exactly on a class boundary, so no
interpolation is involved; so do the \SI{10}{\percent} and \SI{15}{\percent}
thresholds priced in §\ref{sec:sensitivity}. The grid is rendered at
\SI{25}{\metre} and a \SI{100}{\metre} cell is excluded when the \emph{majority}
of its samples are at or above the threshold --- that is, when most of the
hectare a turbine needs is too steep, not when a single steep pixel touches it.
On that rule \SI{37.5}{\percent} of the Walloon Region is excluded, and the
reconstruction passes two independent checks: \SI{99}{\percent} of the cells the
Region's separate $>30^\circ$ vector layer flags are inside it, and its
north/south split (\SI{19.5}{\percent} of the northern third against
\SI{49.3}{\percent} of the southern third) is the Hesbaye--Ardenne gradient.
Among land that passes every other rule, the standing fleet avoids it strongly
(§\ref{sec:calibration}).

\paragraph{Radar and radio astronomy.} skeyes and Defence do not publish
radar-constraint geometry, and the AOEM map covers obstacle-limitation surfaces,
not radar interference. Three installations whose protection perimeters fall
inside Wallonia and are not already covered by an aerodrome ring are
reconstructed as circles at their published coordinates: the Wideumont C-band
weather radar (\SI{5}{\kilo\metre}, WMO/OPERA guidance), the Humain
radio-astronomy station (\SI{3}{\kilo\metre}) and the Bertem primary/secondary
surveillance radar (\SI{10}{\kilo\metre}, of which only the southern arc reaches
Walloon Brabant). Together they remove \ServRadarRemoved{}~\si{\square
\kilo\metre}, so the approximation is not load-bearing. The skeyes and Defence
\emph{red zones} themselves are a different matter: they are published neither
as geometry nor as rules, BREGILAB applies them (§\ref{sec:bregilab}), and they
are the one family whose absence here could matter.

\subsection{The forest species split}\label{sec:forest}

The CoDT excludes a mast standing in a broad-leaved stand. The plan de secteur
records forest zoning but not species, so the split has to come from a land-cover
product. This study uses CORINE grid codes 23/24/25 at \SI{250}{\metre}, 2006
vintage, counting mixed stands as broad-leaved --- the conservative choice, and
the same source PyPSA-Eur uses.

Two better sources were identified and neither can be used:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{CARTOFOR}, the continuous mapping of the Walloon forest by
    Gembloux Agro-Bio~Tech under the Accord-Cadre de recherche forestière, has a
    2016 three-class product distinguishing broad-leaved from coniferous stands.
    Its own documentation states that the cartographic products are undergoing
    validation and will be made available on the Region's portal in due course;
    they are not published today, and no service exposes them.
  \item \textbf{WALOUS 2018}, the Walloon land-cover grid, does carry explicit
    \emph{Feuillus} and \emph{Résineux} classes at a far finer resolution than
    CORINE, and would be the correct implementation of the CoDT rule ---
    including for the broad-leaved woodlots that sit inside agricultural zones,
    which the present implementation misses. Its service answers point queries
    but returns a blank image to the bulk export that the slope grid accepts, so
    it cannot be retrieved as a grid. Obtaining it is an administrative step.
\end{itemize}

\caveat{The stake is bounded. The species split only decides the status of
plan-de-secteur forest zones lying within \SI{750}{\metre} of a PIC, which is a
small share of a Region whose eligible land is overwhelmingly agricultural; and
the direction of the error from using CORINE is not knowable without the better
data, because a coarse raster both admits broad-leaved cells and rejects
coniferous ones.}

\section{PyPSA-Eur's default constraint set is not applicable in Wallonia}
\label{sec:pypsaeur}
% =====================================================================

PyPSA-Eur's onshore-wind land analysis (\texttt{config/config.default.yaml},
section \texttt{renewable.onwind}) admits CORINE grid codes 12--29, 31 and 32,
excludes a \SI{1}{\kilo\metre} buffer around codes 1--6, excludes Natura~2000,
and assumes \SI{3}{\mega\watt\per\square\kilo\metre}. Five features of that
specification make it unsuitable here.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{It admits all forest.} Codes 23, 24 and 25 are broad-leaved,
    coniferous and mixed forest. Wallonia is roughly one third forest, and the
    forest is concentrated in the Ardennes --- the windiest part of the Region.
    Walloon law excludes broad-leaved stands outright and admits coniferous
    stands only near a PIC. This single difference moves the answer more than
    any other.
  \item \textbf{It cannot see scattered housing.} CORINE's minimum mapping unit
    is \SI{25}{\hectare}. Walloon \emph{habitat dispersé} --- isolated farms and
    ribbon development --- is invisible to it, and it is exactly what the
    \SI{400}{\metre} rule protects. The \SI{1}{\kilo\metre} buffer around CORINE
    urban codes is not a substitute: it is generous where settlements are large
    and blind where they are not.
  \item \textbf{It has no zoning.} The plan de secteur is the legal instrument
    that decides admissibility in Wallonia, and CORINE is a land-\emph{cover}
    product that knows nothing about it.
  \item \textbf{Its buffer around transport infrastructure has the wrong sign.}
    PyPSA-Eur excludes \SI{1}{\kilo\metre} around roads and railways; the
    Walloon rule requires agricultural sites to be \emph{within}
    \SI{1.5}{\kilo\metre} of the main ones.
  \item \textbf{The CORINE raster shipped in the data bundle is
    \SI{250}{\metre}, 2006 vintage} (\texttt{g250\_clc06\_V18\_5.tif}). At
    \SI{250}{\metre} a single pixel is \SI{6.25}{\hectare}; at the scale of a
    \SI{400}{\metre} setback that is a coarse instrument, and the land cover is
    twenty years old.
\end{enumerate}

\finding{PyPSA-Eur's default land analysis admits
\BaseEligible{}~\si{\square\kilo\metre} of Wallonia and returns
\BasePnom{}~MW. That figure should not be quoted as a Walloon technical
potential, and the decision to override it in
\texttt{input\_parameters\_for\_models.csv} was correct. What was missing was a
corrected calculation to override it \emph{with}.}

\section{Calibration against the standing fleet}\label{sec:calibration}
% =====================================================================

A land-eligibility study is a claim about where a machine may be built. The
cheapest way to find out whether the claim is calibrated is to point it at the
machines that exist. The Region does not publish turbine coordinates;
OpenStreetMap does, and its coverage of the Belgian fleet is essentially
complete. \FleetN{} turbines are mapped inside the Walloon Region. They are used
only to test the constraint set and the siting rules --- never as an input to
the potential.

\subsection{Which constraints the fleet avoids}

For each constraint layer, Table~\ref{tab:fleet} reports the share of the Region
it covers, the share of the standing fleet it covers, and the ratio of the two.
A ratio well below one says that developers and the permitting authority treat
the constraint as real; a ratio at or above one says that they do not, whatever
the layer is called.

That ratio is reported twice. Against the whole Region, it confounds each layer
with all the others: steep slopes lie mostly in forest that the zoning excludes
anyway, so a slope layer's Region-wide ratio mostly measures the forest. The
\emph{conditional} ratio (columns marked $^*$) counts only the land, and only
the machines, that pass every other layer of the set; it is the one that tests
the layer. The two size-dependent setbacks are left out of the conditions,
because most standing machines are smaller than the reference class.

\subsection{What the addresses around the fleet are}

\FleetAddrN{} standing machines have an ICAR address point within
\SI{400}{\metre}. For \FleetAddrZae{} of them the nearest such address lies in
a zone d'activité économique, and for \FleetAddrAgri{} in an agricultural zone.
The first are businesses, or the operators' housing the 2024 rule exempts; they
are not dwellings in the sense of the rule, and the reference drops the
\AddrExempt{} addresses that lie in those zones. The second are farms, most of
them dwellings. Everywhere else every address is kept, so shops, schools and
barns still count as dwellings: the setback remains mildly conservative.

\subsection{How the fleet is grouped}\label{sec:fleetgeometry}

Grouped by single linkage at \FleetFarmLink{}~km, the \FleetN{} machines form
\FleetFarms{} farms of \FleetPerFarm{} machines on average; \FleetFarmsFour{} of
those farms have four machines or more and hold \FleetInBigFarms{}\,\% of the
fleet; the largest has \FleetLargestFarm{}. Measured the way the Region measures
its inter-distance --- between the nearest masts of two farms --- the distance
from each farm to its nearest neighbour has a p10 of \FleetFarmEdgePTen{}~m, a
p25 of \FleetFarmEdgePTwentyFive{}~m and a median of \FleetFarmEdgePFifty{}~m:
\FleetEdgeBelowFour{}\,\% of the farms are closer than 4~km to another and
\FleetEdgeBelowSix{}\,\% closer than 6~km. \FleetMwFarms{} farms stand entirely
within \MotorwayExempt{}~km of a motorway, where the inter-distance does not
apply; of the others, \FleetNonMwEdgeBelowFour{}\,\% are still closer than 4~km
to a neighbour. Only \FleetNearMotorway{}\,\% of the machines stand within
\SI{1}{\kilo\metre} of a motorway (\FleetNearMotorwayWide{}\,\% within
\SI{1.5}{\kilo\metre}), so the motorway exemption concerns a third of the fleet,
not most of it.

\subsection{The open horizon is observed}\label{sec:horizoncheck}

Of the \HorizonSettlements{} villages, \HorizonAffected{} have a standing
machine within \PlaceHorizonRadius{}~km, and only \HorizonFailing{} of them
(\HorizonFailingPct{}\,\%) have less than \PlaceAzimuth{}° of their horizon
free of turbines; the p1 of the largest free arc is \HorizonArcPOne{}°. The rule
is respected in practice, and the implementation used in the placement model
reproduces that --- it is the same code.

\subsection{The residual allowance}\label{sec:residual}

Two families of the Walloon method have no public geometry and both reduce the
potential. They are represented by a multiplicative allowance on the reference,
with a central value and a bracket:

\begin{description}
  \item[Priority ornithological zones, \ResOrnithology{}\,\% (bracket
    0--20\,\%).] The DEMNA high-priority bird layer is an integral exclusion of
    the favourable-zone method and is not published. It was built largely from
    concentrations of sensitive species, which in Wallonia coincide to a large
    extent with the Natura~2000 bird SPAs and the wetlands already excluded
    here; the incremental area is the non-Natura remainder. The central value is
    a judgement, not a measurement, and it is stated as one.
  \item[Partial constraints, \ResPartial{}\,\% (bracket 0--30\,\%).] The method
    distinguishes integral from \emph{partial} exclusions --- medium-priority
    bird zones, bat-interest zones, migration corridors, the principal
    ecological structure, radar study zones between 8 and \SI{16}{\kilo\metre}
    --- and grants turbines falling in them a 25\,\% success rate. Their
    combined footprint is unknown; the central estimate assumes they cover a
    fifth of the surviving land, so the expected loss is
    $0.20\times0.75 = 15\,\%$.
\end{description}

Together they leave \ResSurvival{}\,\% of the reference in the central case.

\subsection{Full-load hours and energy}

The capacity factor is computed on the 2013 ERA5/SARAH-3 cutout with the same
layout weighting PyPSA-Eur uses. For the reference case it is \AdminFlh{}~h,
which with the reference capacity gives \PlaceEnergy{}~TWh/yr gross and
\ResCentralEnergy{}~TWh/yr after the residual allowance --- against the
\TargetTWh{}~TWh/yr of the PNEC/PACE 2030 target.

Those full-load hours are high, and the reason is the machine, not the method.
The reference class is an NREL~2020~ATB \SI{4}{\mega\watt} turbine with a
\SI{150}{\metre} rotor --- a specific power of
\SI{226}{\watt\per\square\metre}, against \SI{305}{\watt\per\square\metre} for
the V112 PyPSA-Eur uses by default --- on a \SI{110}{\metre} hub instead of
\SI{80}{\metre}. Low-specific-power machines on tall towers are exactly what
raises full-load hours, and it is why the same workflow returns
\VOneTwelveFlh{}~h when the V112 is substituted into the same eligible land. The
V112 number is the one that reconciles with §\ref{sec:validation} and with the
model's own resource.

\caveat{These are \emph{gross} new-turbine full-load hours: no wake, availability
or electrical losses are applied, which is PyPSA-Eur's convention for
\texttt{p\_max\_pu} and therefore the convention PyPSA-Wal needs. They are not
comparable with the 1\,885~h TIMES-WAL uses for the blended existing fleet, nor
with what the standing Walloon fleet delivers; that discrepancy is analysed in
\texttt{docs/renewable-potentials.md}~§10. BREGILAB applies a calibrated
performance ratio of \BregPerf{} to the same kind of figure
(§\ref{sec:bregilab}), which is the right correction for a study reporting
delivered energy.}

\caveat{Only the 2013 weather year is available on this machine. The Walloon
onshore-wind resource in 2013 is about 5\,\% \emph{better} than in 2010, the
year the main model runs use (\texttt{docs/renewable-potentials.md}~§10.1).
Capacity is unaffected --- it depends only on the land --- but the energy column
is optimistic by roughly that margin relative to a 2010 run.}

annual energy --- and decisive for the headline capacity number, which is a
reason to report both.

\section{What the answer depends on}\label{sec:sensitivity}
% =====================================================================

A technical potential is a statement about what the rules permit, and some of
the rules are policy. The Cadre de référence is a circular ``à valeur
indicative'' that a permit may depart from with adequate reasons, and the plan
de secteur itself admits derogations for wind farms. A potential that silently
builds in one reading of a policy rule answers a political question without
saying so. Table~\ref{tab:senscases} therefore prices every choice the reference
rests on, one at a time: each row is the reference with exactly that one change,
through the same eligibility code and the same checked placement model.

\paragraph{Policy rules.} Four rules decide the answer, and none of them is
about the land in the physical sense.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{The agricultural corridor} (CoDT R.II.36-2) keeps turbines within
    \SI{1.5}{\kilo\metre} of the main infrastructure network. It is the rule for
    a permit in conformity with the plan de secteur; the derogation route of
    D.IV.11 is used for about one standing machine in five
    (§\ref{sec:calibration}). Admitting agricultural land wherever the other
    rules allow gives \SensNoCorridorMW{}~MW (\SensNoCorridorPct{}\,\%), on
    \SensNoCorridorArea{}~\si{\square\kilo\metre} of eligible land.
  \item \emph{The minimum park size} of four machines, which the 2024 framework
    itself relaxes for machines above \SI{3.2}{\mega\watt}: without it,
    \SensParksOneMW{}~MW (\SensParksOnePct{}\,\%).
  \item \emph{The inter-distance between parks}, recommended and reducible:
    \SensIdFourMW{}~MW at 4~km (\SensIdFourPct{}\,\%) and \SensIdSixMW{}~MW at
    6~km (\SensIdSixPct{}\,\%).
  \item \emph{The habitat setback}: the 2013 rule of four times the tip height
    instead of the 2024 rule gives \SensHabitatOldMW{}~MW
    (\SensHabitatOldPct{}\,\%).
\end{itemize}

The open-horizon rule belongs to the same family, but it is stated as a result
to be preserved and the standing fleet observes it; dropping it would give
\SensNoHorizonMW{}~MW (\SensNoHorizonPct{}\,\%). Relaxing the corridor and the
park minimum together, with the open horizon kept, gives \SensRelaxedMW{}~MW;
tightening the inter-distance to 6~km and the habitat setback to the 2013 rule
gives \SensTightenedMW{}~MW. \emph{The policy envelope around the gross
reference is therefore \SensTightenedMW{}--\SensRelaxedMW{}~MW}, wider than the
residual-allowance bracket and wider than any modelling choice.

\paragraph{Modelling choices.} Three exclusions are this study's judgement, and
the fleet supports each of them (§\ref{sec:calibration}): the ADESA landscape
inventory, which has no legal force (without it, \SensNoAdesaMW{}~MW,
\SensNoAdesaPct{}\,\%; without any landscape perimeter, \SensNoLandscapeMW{}~MW,
\SensNoLandscapePct{}\,\%); the slope criterion (\SensSlopeTenMW{}~MW at
\SI{10}{\percent}, \SensSlopeFifteenMW{}~MW at \SI{15}{\percent}); and the two
inner aeronautical rings (\SensNoAviationMW{}~MW without them).

\paragraph{A misreading.} Taking every plan-de-secteur road as a PIC --- the
reading BREGILAB appears to share (§\ref{sec:bregilab}) --- would give
\SensPicPdsMW{}~MW (\SensPicPdsPct{}\,\%). That is not a policy choice but a
reading of R.II.21-1, which is explicit, and it is not followed.

\finding{The reference is the framework in force as it is applied: the CoDT
corridor, parks of four, the open horizon, no binding inter-distance. The single
rule that matters most is the agricultural corridor (\SensNoCorridorPct{}\,\%),
a planning rule the Region itself derogates from; the 6~km inter-distance
(\SensIdSixPct{}\,\%), the open horizon (\SensNoHorizonPct{}\,\% if dropped),

(\SensParksOnePct{}\,\%) each move the answer by a quarter to a third. A
reader who wants a potential under a different policy should take it from
Table~\ref{tab:senscases} rather than from the headline.}

\subsection{BREGILAB / VITO Dynamic Energy Atlas}\label{sec:bregilab}

The other published bottom-up estimate of the Walloon onshore-wind potential is
the BREGILAB project's, computed with VITO's Dynamic Energy Atlas (Clymans et
al., 2022, WP3, §5.2.2 and Table~22). Its Table~22 gives Wallonia
\BregCurrent{}~MW current and \BregAdditional{}~MW additional onshore wind; the
\BregTotalGW{}~GW quoted in its running text is their sum, that is, a
\emph{gross} potential on the same convention as \texttt{p\_nom\_max}. It is
$\times\BregVsReference$ this study's gross reference and
$\times\BregVsCentral$ its central estimate. The difference can be traced, and
most of it is not about the land.

\paragraph{What BREGILAB computes.} It delineates ``available space'' on a
\SI{100}{\metre} grid from the 2013 cadre de référence, the CoDT and the 2009
ICEDD study --- its WTN scenario, Tables~20--21 --- and then runs an allocation
algorithm that packs in as many Vestas V112 (3.3~MW, hub \SI{94}{\metre}) as
will fit at a minimum spacing of $5D = \SI{560}{\metre}$. Its own Figure~21
shows machines allocated on isolated pixels of available space: the algorithm is
a free allocation in the sense of §\ref{sec:placement}. Constraints are combined
by a weighted overlay with an exclusion threshold of 0.5; the Walloon landscape
rules --- the 130° open horizon and the inter-distance, quoted verbatim in its
Table~20 --- enter only as a layer of ``protected views (4~km) around urban
settlements'' with a weight of 0.4, which by construction excludes nothing on its
own. Production is corrected by a performance ratio of \BregPerf{}, calibrated
against Elia's measured output --- which is why its reported availability factor
for the Walloon potential is \BregFlh{}~h and not the \BregFlhGross{}~h implied
gross, a figure close to what this workflow gets for the same machine, and a
useful independent check that the two resource models agree.

\paragraph{Its rules on open data.} To see whether the land explains the
number, the workflow rebuilds BREGILAB's WTN constraint set on this study's
data: the 2013 habitat setback of four times the \SI{150}{\metre} tip height,
\SI{400}{\metre} from dwellings, its negative advice limits around roads,
railways, waterways and HV lines, \SI{2}{\kilo\metre} around the nuclear sites,
\SI{560}{\metre} around the standing fleet, Natura~2000 and the reserves, forest
and green zoning, heritage, high-risk karst, the aeronautical no-go rings
standing in for its aviation red zones, and its \SI{1.5}{\kilo\metre} corridor
along the ``road, rail and waterway network''. It admits
\BregEmuArea{}~\si{\square\kilo\metre}, and a free allocation of its machine on
that land, with the standing fleet kept, gives \BregEmuMW{}~MW. BREGILAB
publishes \BregTotal{}~MW, \BregReportedShare{}\,\% of that. The available
space its Figure~20 maps for Wallonia is visibly sparser than its own rules
produce on open data; the layers that could account for the difference ---
skeyes and Defence red zones, SEVESO and pipeline buffers, cadastral
residential parcels instead of address points, undeveloped industrial parcels ---
are listed in its tables but not published, so its figure cannot be reproduced
from its documentation.

study's data and adds this study's rules one at a time, all with BREGILAB's
machine and spacing, then switches to the reference machine and to the park and
landscape rules.

\paragraph{Why BREGILAB's number is not a technical potential under Walloon
rules.} Three things separate it from this study's answer, and they do not
point the same way.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{It is a land-and-wake bound.} It places machines one pixel at a
    time, forms no parks and applies neither the park definition nor the
    open-horizon rule of the Cadre. On this study's land those two rules take
    the land-and-wake bound from \FreePnom{}~MW to \PlacePnom{}~MW. This is the
    main reason its headline is high, and it is a matter of method, not of
    data.
  \item \textbf{Its documented constraint set is looser than the Walloon
    rules.} It does not exclude the landscape perimeters, slopes or flood zones,
    and its corridor follows every road rather than the CoDT's PIC network. On
    its own machine, this study's rules take its constraint set from
    \BregEmuGrossMW{}~MW to \BregBridgeEndMW{}~MW (Table~\ref{tab:bregilab}).
  \item \textbf{Its implemented constraint set is tighter than its
    documentation.} Its published figure is only \BregReportedShare{}\,\% of
    what its own rules give on open data, which is what the undocumented layers
    remove.
\end{enumerate}

The second and third effects pull in opposite directions and happen to cancel:
BREGILAB's \BregTotal{}~MW sits close to this study's land-and-wake bound of
\FreePnom{}~MW. The agreement is a coincidence, not a confirmation, and the
factor $\times\BregVsReference$ between BREGILAB and this study's gross
reference is the first effect: BREGILAB counts the machines that fit, this study
counts the machines the Walloon rules allow to be arranged in parks.

\paragraph{What was adopted from it.} Three things, and together they changed
the answer of this study more than any new data layer did:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Allocating machines instead of multiplying area by a density.}
    This is BREGILAB's method, it is better than an area calculation on
    fragmented land, and it removes the need for any patch-size screen.
  \item \textbf{Graded rather than binary constraints.} BREGILAB's weighting of
    consultation zones is the idea behind this study's separate treatment of the
    aeronautical rings and behind the residual allowance for partial
    constraints.
  \item \textbf{Validating against measured reality.} BREGILAB calibrates its
    production against Elia. This study cannot do that for a potential, but the
    same instinct produced the fleet calibration of §\ref{sec:calibration}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What was not adopted, and why.} BREGILAB's performance ratio of
\BregPerf{} is a calibration factor that absorbs both physical losses and the
bias of its own wind model against Elia's measurements. Applying it to a
PyPSA-Eur profile would double-count, because PyPSA-Wal expects a gross
\texttt{p\_max\_pu}. Its weighted-overlay treatment of soft constraints was also
not reproduced literally: with only two soft families left unmapped here, an
explicit allowance with a stated bracket (§\ref{sec:residual}) is more
transparent than a hidden weight --- and a weight of 0.4 on the landscape rules
is, in effect, a decision not to apply them.

\subsection{The 2022 SPW/Gembloux favourable-zone update}

That study reports \SpwStrictArea{}~\si{\square\kilo\metre} of \emph{zones
favorables strictes} and \SpwEnlargedArea{}~\si{\square\kilo\metre} of
\emph{zones favorables élargies} for its ``180~m'' case, and \SpwNewGWh{}~GWh/yr
of \emph{additional} production from 457 potential turbines, cut down from 1\,323
simulated ones by landscape inter-distance and encirclement criteria.

Three differences are structural:

\begin{itemize}
  \item It applies the priority bird zones, which this study represents only
    by an allowance, and it applies the landscape inter-distance and the
    encirclement examination --- the criteria that cut its own simulation from
    1\,323 turbines to 457 --- where this study keeps the open horizon and treats
    the inter-distance as a sensitivity.
  \item It counts only \emph{new} capacity, excluding the footprint of the
    standing fleet, whereas this study reports a gross potential.
  \item It applies a wind-resource threshold; this study applies none.
\end{itemize}

Adding the standing fleet back to its 457 turbines gives of order
\SI{4}{\giga\watt} on a comparable gross basis, which is where this study lands
once the inter-distance is applied as a rule (\InterdistLow{}--\InterdistHigh{}~MW)
--- the reading of the landscape rules that the 2022 update made.

\subsection{The model's own cap}

\CapToday{}~MW against this study's gross reference of \PlacePnom{}~MW is a
factor of \CapRatio{} --- the cap is, to within a percent, the gross
reference --- and against its central estimate of \ResCentral{}~MW a factor of
\CapRatioCentral{}. Two qualifications matter.

First, the cap was constructed as a deployment expectation --- what is plausible
by 2030 plus the project pipeline --- and this report produces a land-and-siting
envelope. That the two land in the same range is a coincidence of construction,
not a confirmation.

Second, whether the cap is inside the envelope depends on the policy choices of
§\ref{sec:sensitivity}: it is reachable under the reference rules only if none
of the unmapped constraint families binds, it is out of reach under a binding
inter-distance, and it is comfortably inside the envelope on the derogation route
for agricultural land.

\subsection{Three independent cross-checks}\label{sec:crosschecks}

The conclusion of §\ref{sec:answer} rests on a chain --- constraint set,
eligible raster, machine allocation, park allocation, residual allowance ---
and an error anywhere in it would move the answer. Three checks test the end of
the chain against things that are known independently of it.

\paragraph{The layout obeys its own rules.} Every layout in this report is
checked against the rules it claims to satisfy by an implementation separate
from the one that produced it --- spacing, park size, inter-distance and open
horizon --- and the workflow fails if one is broken
(§\ref{sec:answer}).

\paragraph{The parks.} The reference places \PlaceParks{} parks against the
\FleetFarms{} farms that exist, that is, it says Wallonia could host somewhat
more parks than it has, most of them larger. The median park has
\PlaceMedianPark{} machines against a fleet average of \FleetPerFarm{}, and the
tail is long --- the largest has \PlaceLargestPark{} machines against
\FleetLargestFarm{} in the fleet --- because nothing but the open horizon limits
a park's extent. The inter-distance sensitivities remove parks rather than
shrink them: at 6~km the model keeps \IdSixParks{} parks.

\paragraph{The resulting deployment density, against a country that has built
one.} Germany ended 2024 with \SI{63.55}{\giga\watt} of onshore wind in
\num{28717} machines on \SI{357600}{\square\kilo\metre} --- a deployed density of
\DeDensity{}~kW/\si{\square\kilo\metre} and \DeTurbines{} machines per
\SI{1000}{\square\kilo\metre}. Wallonia today carries
\WalDensityToday{}~kW/\si{\square\kilo\metre}, \TodayVsDe{} times that. This
study's central estimate corresponds to
\WalDensityCentral{}~kW/\si{\square\kilo\metre}, \CentralVsDe{} times Germany's
current deployed density, and the top of its range to \HighVsDe{} times.

\finding{A technical potential that lands at \CentralVsDe{} times the density
Germany has \emph{already built} is in a credible place: above what Wallonia has
done, not far above what a comparable country has done, and well below the
densities reached in Schleswig-Holstein or Lower Saxony.}

\caveat{The allocations are greedy, not optimal: parks are grown from the best
seeds first and never revisited. An exact packing would fit somewhat more, so
every placement figure is a slight under-estimate of what the rules permit ---
in the opposite direction to every other approximation in this report.}

\section{Hypotheses}\label{sec:hypotheses}
% =====================================================================

Every choice below is a config entry, every one can be varied, and each is
stated with the direction in which it moves the result; the policy and
judgement choices are priced in Table~\ref{tab:senscases}.

\begin{description}
  \item[Region.] The administrative Walloon Region, not the model's
    \texttt{BEWAL} node (§\ref{sec:region}). \emph{A \texttt{BEWAL} figure would
    be about \SI{10}{\percent} lower.}
  \item[Capacity comes from placing machines and parks,] not from multiplying
    area by a density (§\ref{sec:placement}). \emph{The density convention would
    give \AdminPnom{}~MW for reasons that have nothing to do with the rules.}
  \item[Minimum inter-turbine distance] \PlaceRotorD{}\,$D$ = \PlaceSpacing{}~m,
    between any two machines, the nearest-neighbour distance of a $5D\times7D$
    array and the rule VITO uses. \emph{The standing Walloon fleet sits at
    \SpObsD{}\,$D$ (\SpObsMW{}~MW); the area-equivalent $\sqrt{5\times7}\,D$
    would give \SpIsoMW{}~MW.}
  \item[Parks] grown at the fleet's \PlaceLink{}~km linkage, at least
    \PlaceMinTurbines{} machines, no radius and no centre separation.
    \emph{Without the minimum, \ParksOnePnom{}~MW.}
  \item[Open horizon] \PlaceAzimuth{}° free within \PlaceHorizonRadius{}~km of
    every village above \SI{10}{\hectare}, as the Cadre states it and as the
    fleet observes it. \emph{\HorizonCostPct{}\,\% of the parks figure.}
  \item[Inter-distance between parks] not binding in the reference; 4 and 6~km
    between nearest masts, motorways exempt, as sensitivities.
    \emph{\IdFourMW{} and \IdSixMW{}~MW.}
  \item[Agricultural corridor] \SI{1.5}{\kilo\metre} from the PIC network of
    R.II.21-1 or a zone d'activité économique. \emph{The derogation route gives
    \SensNoCorridorMW{}~MW.}
  \item[Habitat setback] the 2024 framework, $500 + H/2$. \emph{The 2013 rule
    ($4H$) gives \SensHabitatOldMW{}~MW.}
  \item[Dwellings] \SI{400}{\metre} around every ICAR address point outside the
    zones d'activité économique. The address layer includes non-residential
    buildings, so this is mildly conservative; the 2022 SPW study makes the same
    approximation.
  \item[Infrastructure distances] roads $1.5\times$ blade length, railways
    \SI{50}{\metre}, high-speed lines \SI{190}{\metre}, HV lines $1.5\,D$: the
    exclusion distances of 2013, now consultation distances. \emph{Weakly
    avoided by the fleet; conservative.}
  \item[Aeronautical servitudes] the AOEM rings at 0 and \SI{15}{\metre} only,
    after the fleet calibration of §\ref{sec:calibration}. \emph{Without them,
    \SensNoAviationMW{}~MW.}
  \item[Slope] $\geq 7\,\%$ on the ERRUISSOL \SI{10}{\metre} grid, majority rule
    over the \SI{25}{\metre} samples in each \SI{100}{\metre} cell. \emph{At
    \SI{15}{\percent}, \SensSlopeFifteenMW{}~MW.}
  \item[Landscape perimeters] the plan-de-secteur PIP and the ADESA inventory,
    both excluded. \emph{Without ADESA, \SensNoAdesaMW{}~MW.}
  \item[Karst] the \emph{contrainte forte} level only.
  \item[Radar and radio astronomy] three circles at published coordinates with
    generic separation distances (§\ref{sec:newlayers}). \emph{Worth
    \ServRadarRemoved{}~\si{\square\kilo\metre}, so not load-bearing.} The skeyes
    and Defence red zones are not represented at all. \emph{Reduces, by an
    unknown amount.}
  \item[Broad-leaved / coniferous split] CORINE at \SI{250}{\metre}, mixed
    stands counted as broad-leaved; CARTOFOR and WALOUS would both be better and
    neither is retrievable (§\ref{sec:forest}). \emph{Direction unknown.}
  \item[Residual allowance] \ResOrnithology{}\,\% for priority bird zones and
    \ResPartial{}\,\% for partial constraints, both bracketed
    (§\ref{sec:residual}). \emph{Both reduce; the brackets are carried into the
    headline range.}
  \item[Runoff flood hazard] excluded from the flood layer; the overflow extent
    at the extreme return period is used instead.
  \item[Geometry generalisation] heavy polygon layers are requested with
    \SI{25}{\metre} server-side generalisation --- a quarter of the exclusion
    raster cell, so it cannot change the aggregate.
  \item[Existing wind farms are not excluded.] The result is a \emph{gross}
    potential: the standing \Installed{}~MW occupies land that is counted as
    available. This is the right convention for \texttt{p\_nom\_max}, which caps
    the total fleet, not additions.
  \item[Repowering is not a separate term.] A land-and-siting study measures the
    envelope, not the turnover of the fleet inside it. EDORA's additional
    3.8~GW by 2050 explicitly includes repowering, which is part of why that
    number and this one are not the same kind of quantity.
  \item[No grid-connection or economic screen.] Distance to the HV network,
    connection cost and wind-speed profitability thresholds are not applied.
    \emph{All reduce.}
  \item[Energy is gross.] No wake, availability or electrical losses, matching
    PyPSA-Eur's \texttt{p\_max\_pu} convention. \emph{A delivered-energy figure
    would be roughly \SI{15}{\percent} lower; BREGILAB's calibrated performance
    ratio is \BregPerf{}.}
  \item[Weather year 2013.] Capacity is unaffected; energy is about 5\,\%
    optimistic relative to the 2010 year the model runs use.
\end{description}

% =====================================================================
\section{What this means for \texttt{p\_nom\_max}}
% =====================================================================

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The cap can be documented rather than cited.} This report gives
    a gross reference of \PlacePnom{}~MW and a central estimate of
    \ResCentral{}~MW under the framework in force as it is applied, and prices
    every policy rule around them. Replacing the CSV's ``PNEC wallon et EDORA''
    with that derivation --- and with the policy reading it carries --- removes
    the main weakness of the current number.
  \item \textbf{If a single value is wanted, \ResCentral{}~MW is the defensible
    one} for a potential under present rules. \CapToday{}~MW is above it by a
    factor of \CapRatioCentral{}; it is reachable within the present rules only
    if the unmapped constraint families do not bind, and within the policy
    envelope of §\ref{sec:sensitivity} on the derogation route for agricultural
    land.
  \item \textbf{State the policy reading with the number.} A potential under a
    binding 4--6~km inter-distance (\InterdistLow{}--\InterdistHigh{}~MW) and one
    on the derogation route (\SensNoCorridorMW{}~MW gross) are both defensible
    readings of Walloon rules; they differ by more than the residual-allowance
    bracket, and PyPSA-Wal should say which one it uses.
  \item \textbf{Settle the region question before quoting anything externally.}
    A Region figure applied to the \texttt{BEWAL} node overstates the node by
    about \SI{10}{\percent} (§\ref{sec:region}).
  \item \textbf{Decide what \texttt{p\_nom\_max} is meant to be.} PyPSA-Wal
    already carries a build-rate limit and a 2030 corridor
    (\texttt{docs/renewable-potentials.md}~§3--4) to represent deployment. If
    those are doing their job, \texttt{p\_nom\_max} should be the technical
    potential and the present value is the wrong kind of number in the right
    range. That is a modelling decision, not a GIS one.
\end{enumerate}

% =====================================================================
\section{What would sharpen the number further}
% =====================================================================

None of these is a gap that invalidates the result; each would narrow the range.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Ask SPW Énergie for the DEMNA priority-bird layer and the
    partial-constraint layers.} They exist and the favourable-zone cartography
    uses them. They would replace the residual allowance, which is the widest
    single term in the range, with geometry.
  \item \textbf{Ask skeyes and Defence for their wind-turbine red zones.} They
    are the one integral constraint family this study cannot represent and
    BREGILAB can, and they are the most likely explanation of the gap between
    BREGILAB's rules and its published figure.
  \item \textbf{Ask SPW Territoire how the inter-distance and the derogation
    route are applied.} Where the 4~km and 6~km figures are reduced in
    practice, and how often agricultural land away from the PIC network is
    permitted, are the two policy questions the answer depends on most; a map
    of short- and long-view landscapes would let the inter-distance be applied
    at the right value in each.
  \item \textbf{Request WALOUS 2018 as a grid, or CARTOFOR when it is
    published.} Either would replace a 2006 \SI{250}{\metre} raster with a
    Walloon product and would implement the CoDT broad-leaved rule where it
    currently is not applied at all (§\ref{sec:forest}).
  \item \textbf{Repeat on the 2010 weather year} once its cutout is available,
    to put the energy column on the same footing as the model runs.
\end{enumerate}
