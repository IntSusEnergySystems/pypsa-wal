I found only a few places with a binding minimum number of turbines, and none with a general ban on single turbines. Where binding minimums exist they are 3 turbines or more, or were set by one province, and they usually lapse in industrial zones or along major infrastructure. The overall trend is towards relaxing these rules. Most places use a preference for clustering or leave it to case-by-case assessment.

1. Wallonia
2013 Cadre (approved 21 Feb 2013, amended 11 Jul 2013), p. 12. It stated a priority, not a minimum. Verbatim: "Les parcs se composant d'un minimum de 5 éoliennes seront prioritaires". Smaller parks "seront autorisés", provided they limit mitage and do not reduce the zone's overall potential. It adds that "la priorité va au groupement des unités de production, plutôt qu'à la dispersion d'éoliennes individuelles". Archived official PDF: http://web.archive.org/web/20220119013826id_/https://energie.wallonie.be/servlet/Repository/cdr.pdf?ID=28134
A recent official opinion on a single turbine. The Pôle Aménagement du territoire gave an unfavourable opinion on 16 Jan 2026 (AT.26.2.AV) on one Luminus turbine of 4.0–6.8 MW at Nivelles Nord. Its reason: "l'implantation d'une seule éolienne ne permet pas d'optimaliser l'exploitation du potentiel venteux de la zone". It found that the >3.2 MW exception did not apply. An earlier 2.99 MW single-turbine application there had been refused, mainly for under-using the wind resource. https://www.cesewallonie.be/sites/default/files/uploads/avis/AT.26.2.AV%20PU-1%C3%A9olienne-NivellesNord-NIVELLES.pdf
Large single turbines do exist, mostly in business zones:
Nivelles, Rossel print works: 2.35 MW, running since Oct 2020, permitting took about 4.5 years. https://press.luminus.be/les-journaux-du-groupe-rossel-encore-un-peu-plus-verts-grace-a-luminus
Hauts-Sarts, Liège: 2.7 MW for NRB. La Libre (2019) calls this "encore assez rare" in Wallonia and common in Flanders. https://lalibre.be/regions/liege/une-premiere-eolienne-dans-le-zoning-des-hauts-sarts-5c3ca54b9978e2710eb13bde
My own count on the repo's OSM layer (652 machines, 1.5 km single linkage, same as your fleet section). 42 of the 134 groups are singletons, i.e. 6.4 % of machines. There are 13 pairs and 7 triples. Querying Overpass for the singletons' tags: at least 6 are tagged 1.5–2.99 MW, 12 are tagged small, and most are untagged. This is indicative only.
EDORA says it helped obtain "une diminution du nombre minimum d'éoliennes par parc" in 2024. https://www.rapportannuel.edora.org/activites-dossiers/eolien/pax_eolienica/
I found no ministerial statistic on single turbines. The 2022 parliamentary answer gives 144 large installations but no breakdown by size. https://www.parlement-wallonie.be/pwpages?p=interp-questions-voir&type=28&iddoc=109952

Bottom line: it was a preference in 2013. In 2024 it became a 4-turbine definition with a >3.2 MW exception, and that exception is enforced through the condition that the project must not reduce the zone's wind potential.

2. Flanders
RO/2014/02 (25 Apr 2014), which replaced RO/2006/02. Verbatim: "Het is niet aangewezen verschillende individuele turbines verspreid in te planten. Vanaf drie windturbines wordt van een cluster gesproken." https://omgeving.vlaanderen.be/sites/default/files/2021-10/ozb_2014_02_wts.pdf
OMG/2024/1. It drops the clustering preference. Its reasons: strict clustering would "zware hypotheek leggen" on the remaining potential, and sites in industrial zones or along linear infrastructure typically come as applications for one or two turbines. It also cites a Raad van State ruling of 11 May 2023 that the circular binds the administration unless a deviation is justified. https://themis.vlaanderen.be/files/65c4ebec4117e4000d0006c8/download?name=VR+2024+0902+MED.0054-2+Omzendbrief+windturbine+-+bijlage.pdf&content-disposition=inline
OMG/2025/1 (17 Jul 2025), the current circular. Caveat: I read the annex to the government decision, whose date field is blank, not the signed version.
Step 1, within existing built-up land: "Solitaire windturbines zijn daarbij toegestaan."
Step 2, next to large infrastructure: clustered or solitary turbines.
Step 3, open land: clustered parks only, in principle ≥20 MW, "met minimaal drie windturbines".
Annex: https://themis.vlaanderen.be/files/954d6d90-5da2-11f0-a71f-c9eca0a02c79/download?name=VR+2025+1407+MED.0282-2+Omzendbrief+Windturbines+-+bijlage+BIS.pdf&content-disposition=inline
Official notice: https://omgeving.vlaanderen.be/nl/omzendbrief-omv20251-afwegingskader-en-randvoorwaarden-voor-de-oprichting-van-windturbines

Bottom line: single turbines are explicitly allowed in industrial zones and along infrastructure; a minimum of 3 applies only in open land, through a circular that binds the administration itself.

3. Netherlands
National. The NOVI (national spatial vision) "kiest voor grootschalige clustering". Measured with clusters defined as ≥2 turbines within 700 m, 12 % of Dutch turbines were solitary in 2023, against 16 % in 2013. https://www.clo.nl/indicatoren/nl301001-landschappelijke-inpassing-van-windturbines-in-clusters-en-open-landschappen-2013-2023
Friesland.
Windstreek 2000: "Solitaire turbines zijn (met uitzondering van de bedrijventerreinen) uitgesloten". Clusters of 2–10 turbines were allowed in exchange for removing old turbines. Source is the Atelier Fryslân report (2009), a secondary account. https://www.fryslan.frl/_flysystem/media/windenergie.pdf
The 2014 ordinance banned new turbines (I saw this only in summaries).
Amendment of 14 Jul 2020, read verbatim: a new solitary turbine of at most 100 m tip height is allowed only to replace several old solitary turbines of ≥45 m. Up to 3 turbines of ≤15 m hub height are allowed at farms, and village turbines ("dorpsmolens") may be upscaled. https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/prb-2020-4485.pdf
Zeeland (binding ordinance, art. 18.17). New turbines outside the designated concentration locations must stand "in een cluster of een lijnopstelling van ten minste drie windturbines". Inside those locations there is no minimum count. https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR709135
Noord-Holland.
The 2019 regulation (art. 32) largely banned new turbines. From a summary I did not read verbatim, repowering required ≥6 turbines in a line. https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR323343/11
The 2020 guidance treats a solitary turbine as a legitimate layout type, with no other turbines within 10× hub height. https://www.noord-holland.nl/bestanden/pdf/Ruimtelijke%20handreiking%20wind%20op%20land%20OV%20NH2020.pdf

Bottom line: binding minimums exist in some provinces, set at 3 (Zeeland) or through restrictions on new solitary turbines (Friesland). There is no national rule.

4. France
The rule. Article 90 of the Grenelle II law (loi n° 2010-788, 12 Jul 2010) made the feed-in tariff under L.314-1 of the Code de l'énergie conditional on "un nombre de machines électrogènes au moins égal à cinq". Per the Sénat committee report: https://www.senat.fr/rap/a12-051/a12-0515.html
Its purpose, per a university course page: "un seuil minimal de cinq éoliennes par parc … pour lutter contre le mitage". https://uved.univ-perp.fr/module4/co/4%20-%20loi%20Grenelle%20II.html
The rule was a condition for the feed-in tariff, not a ban on permits.
Abolition. The loi Brottes (loi n° 2013-312, 15 Apr 2013, published in the JO on 16 Apr) removed it. Article 24 abolished the ZDE wind zones. https://www.atd31.fr/fr/base-doc/environnement/energie/loi-2013-0005.html
The ministerial answer to AN question 17912 confirms the end of "regroupements d'éoliennes d'au moins 5 mâts". https://questions.assemblee-nationale.fr/q14/14-17912QE.htm
Not verified: which final article deleted the five-mast wording. Légifrance blocked access. The Sénat report places it in bill article 15, merged with 12 bis, so article 24 is likely but unconfirmed.

Bottom line: France had a binding minimum of 5 for less than 3 years, then abolished it as a brake on deployment.

5. Germany
I found no federal or Länder rule on the number of turbines per project. WindBG sets area targets only; I checked this against its structure, not clause by clause.
EIA thresholds. The UVPG defines a "Windfarm" as 3–5 turbines (site-specific screening), 6–19 (general screening) and ≥20 (mandatory EIA). https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uvpg/anlage_1.html
Regional plans steer through area size. Augsburg's draft plan (Nov 2024) designates areas of at least 10 ha and aims at the "Vermeidung von Einzelanlagenstandorten" to avoid "Verspargelung". That is a stated goal, not a cap on project size. https://www.regierung.schwaben.bayern.de/mam/aufgaben/b2/sg_24/regionalplanung/augsburg/4ae-we/rp_9_b_iv_2-4-2_nutzung_der_windenergie_begruendung_der_festlegungen_entwurf_2024-11-13.pdf
How common single turbines are: I could not verify this properly. An industry database lists 30,369 turbines in 11,998 "Windparks" (about 2.5 per park), with no definition of a park. w3.windmesse.de/windenergie/news/37890-g-a-m-e-datenbank-onshore-windpark-da…dte-marktforschung-in-der-energiewirtschaft

Bottom line: Germany steers clustering through area designation and has no minimum turbine count.

6. Denmark, UK, Ireland, Luxembourg
Denmark. Areas must be sized to the "vindmøllegruppe eller enkeltstående vindmølle" (group or single turbine) planned. Groups must form an easily read geometric pattern, and there is no minimum (Naturstyrelsen guidance, 2015). https://videnomvind.dk/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Planl%C3%A6g_vejledning_06012015_web.pdf
Ireland. The 2006 guidelines say "Wind energy proposals may be for single turbines or for groupings of turbines". https://www.opr.ie/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/2006-Wind-Energy-Development-1.pdf
UK.
Northern Ireland, May 2014: 770 renewable applications, 644 of them for individual turbines, driven by subsidies. https://www.niassembly.gov.uk/assembly-business/official-report/committee-minutes-of-evidence/session-2013-2014/july-2014/inquiry-into-wind-energy-department-of-the-environment-officials
Scotland: NatureScot guidance covers single turbines and groups of 2–3 of 15–50 m, and warns of landscape "clutter". https://www.nature.scot/sites/default/files/2019-10/Guidance%20-%20Siting%20and%20Design%20of%20Small%20Scale%20Wind%20Turbines%20of%20between%2015%20and%2050%20metres%20in%20height.pdf
Luxembourg. A "parc" means ≥2 turbines above 100 kVA, which matters for the EIA. A single turbine still needs an environmental ("commodo") permit, and siting is case by case. https://environnement.public.lu/fr/support/faqs/faq-eoliennes.html

Bottom line: none of these four sets a minimum, and single turbines are an accepted category.

7. Arguments
For clustering:
Landscape and scattered development: the 2013 Walloon Cadre, Grenelle II, Augsburg; the Atelier Fryslân report says "Solitairen vormen een zwerm" (single turbines form a swarm).
Using the wind resource fully: the Nivelles opinion.
Economies of scale. NREL 2024 puts a single 1.5 MW turbine at $3,362/kW and $80/MWh, against $1,968/kW and $42/MWh for a 200 MW, 61-turbine plant. Turbine size and hub height are confounded in that comparison. https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy25osti/91775.pdf
Against a minimum:
Fragmented land and industrial or roadside plots only fit 1–2 turbines.
Larger turbines mean fewer per site (Flanders OMG/2024/1).
Village and farm turbines (the Friesland dorpsmolens).
The downside: in Northern Ireland, subsidies led to a proliferation of small single turbines.
Conclusion

A binding "no single turbines" rule is not common in Europe. Binding minimums exist only in Zeeland (≥3, outside concentration zones), in Flanders' open land (≥3, by circular), in Friesland's restrictions on new solitary turbines, and in France's 5-turbine rule, which lasted from 2010 to 2013. Everywhere else it is a preference or nothing, and industrial zones and infrastructure corridors are usually exempt. Wallonia's own 2024 exception for machines above 3.2 MW makes a hard 4-turbine exclusion stricter than the Walloon text itself. A single-turbine sensitivity, or allowing single turbines on patches too small for a park, is well supported. The "ne réduise pas le potentiel" condition justifies still ruling them out where a park would fit.
